# Chapter 7 records review: verdicts to apply

## Block NEU-101..133 (30 ok, 3 claim defects). Zero arithmetic errors.

### The refutation that matters most, and it overturns something I told the user
**NEU-122 — scope too wide, and its note asserts a result the source does not
report.** This is the record the earlier research pass offered as the thing that
undercuts the chapter's "the phenotype is the ratio rather than either isoform".
**It does not.**

- The intervention is a **3R-to-4R splice-switching oligonucleotide**, which
  **lowers 3R as it raises 4R**. The paper calls the outcome "4R tau dominance"
  and "an increase in 4R tau", **never an isolated 4R increase.** So the claim's
  "raising 4R on its own" is wrong, and **the record's own note concedes the
  comparison is a ratio shift in both arms**, contradicting its own claim.
- **Total tau unchanged is reported only for the mutant-line arm**, not the
  oligonucleotide arm. In the oligonucleotide arm total tau was blotted but **no
  result for it is stated anywhere in the text or the caption**; only 4R tau and
  phospho-tau are reported as raised.

**Consequence: the chapter's ratio framing is not undercut by this experiment.
The experiment is itself a ratio shift, which is consistent with it.** NEU-122's
claim and note both need rewriting, and the chapter must not lean on it as the
counter-evidence.

### Two more claim defects
- **NEU-113 — proposal as finding.** The pull-down percentages are measured; the
  regulatory role is the authors' inference, "suggesting that these factors may
  regulate ... via a direct interaction". **And the paper's only regulation
  evidence sits in a figure caption that the record deliberately excludes**, so
  the claim asserts a mechanism the record itself declines to anchor.
- **NEU-101 — scope too wide.** The review names inclusions **only for Pick
  disease**. For the other two it says only that the ratio changes and 4R is
  up-regulated in the majority of cases, **and names no assay**, so the note's
  blanket "the assay is inclusion composition by staining" is unsupported for two
  of the three diseases.

### Two stale independence notes, stale in the dangerous direction
**NEU-116 and NEU-117's notes say independence "cannot be assessed".** The
Crossref-filled bylines now in the records **share three name strings.** Per the
corpus caveat that is a name string rather than a verified laboratory, **but it
is emphatically not an open question, and the chapter must not present these two
as independent.**

**NEU-128's note is incomplete**: it names one chapter-2 record as sharing the
senior author, and **a second chapter-2 record shares it too.** The leads name
both; the record does not.

### Confirmed, and these are the chapter's good material
- **NEU-109 and NEU-110 are both exact, and each measurement is correctly
  identified**: one transcript-level with its test statistic and the explicit "no
  significant difference in exon 10", the other protein-level on the same tissue
  and the same individuals, with every ratio, p-value and N.
- **NEU-111 and NEU-112 are exact, and the hazard is real.** The review's
  N-terminal percentages at **NEU-001's own anchor** assign 54 per cent to the
  class the long reads put under 3 per cent, and the labels look transposed.
  **NEU-001's claim text carries only the 3R:4R sentence, so NEU-001 has no
  defect**, but a writer who opens that anchor finds the bad percentages one
  clause away.
- **The seeding asymmetry is one laboratory**, seven shared authors, both bylines
  in the corpus.
- **The modern tissue comparison is a reanalysis of existing long-read data**, not
  new sequencing, over nine tissues that **exclude testis**, with its thresholds
  and gene counts exact.
- **NEU-124 and NEU-125 are exact in every arm, interval, dose, denominator and
  timepoint**, and the tau-lowering mechanism is anchored **more directly than the
  records say**, in the registration's own description. **So that drug must not be
  listed beside the splice-modulating ones.** Three caveats: the corpus document
  **nowhere labels an outcome primary**, so "the primary endpoint" is an inference
  from position; an age range comes from the title rather than the cited line; and
  one record writes "post-hoc" where the source says "exploratory" throughout.

### Hedges flagged
NEU-107's lead hardens "may explain" into what makes the isoform the determining
variable. NEU-127's lead contrast is the record's rather than the paper's, since
that tissue has **both** the highest total and the largest unique set.

### Mechanical
- **NEU-126 and NEU-127 carry no tier statement at all**, not one of the three
  strings, not a descriptive tier, not "no tier applies".
- **One year conflict**: corpus metadata and the bibkey say 2023, the record says
  2024.
- **A cluster of anchor gaps where a true statement rests on an uncited line**:
  NEU-115, NEU-126, NEU-128, NEU-124. Same class as the seven found in chapter 4
  and the 28 an agent found in chapter 9.
- The dispatch's count of preprints with no published version **does not
  correspond to anything in this block**; all nine preprint flags are correct and
  all nine record a located journal DOI.

## Block NEU-141..179 (24 ok, 15 with defects). Zero arithmetic errors again.

### The pattern: independence notes are the least reliable field in this project
**Four notes in one block of 39 are wrong about who wrote what**, and three of
the four are wrong in the direction that would let the chapter present dependent
work as independent.

- **NEU-157's note says "a different pipeline and a different cell model".
  Both are the same.** Same splicing analyser, same custom workflow, same
  engineered cell line, same knockdown method. **The counts differ because of
  event definition and library type**, which is a different and better sentence.
- **NEU-160's note says "these authors share no author". They share three**, two
  of them visible in the byline of the version actually read.
- **NEU-166's note says the work referred to shares authors with neither paper.
  The work referred to IS NEU-157's paper**, by its own reference number. **A
  chapter reading NEU-157 and NEU-166 as two confirmations would double-count one
  paper.**
- **NEU-179's note names four groups it shares no author with. Two of the four
  are wrong**, including a senior author who appears in the byline of the version
  read.

**And one note is wrong in the safe direction**: NEU-175 names five shared
authors where one of the five is not an author of that paper. Four are, so the
conclusion holds.

**Consequence for the project, not just the chapter.** `check_citations.py`
indexes claim, topic and title and **not** `note:`, so none of this is reachable
by any gate. This is the fourth chapter where an independence statement in a note
turned out to be wrong, and the lesson recorded earlier stands: **a qualifier
that lives only in a note needs its own claim.**

### Claims that overstate or invert their sources
- **NEU-169 — the claim is backwards.** It says the cryptic exons "carry splice
  sites close in strength to those of their annotated neighbours". **The source
  says the canonical sites are on average significantly stronger**, and that the
  cryptic ones are **far above random**, so "real, albeit weaker". The honest
  sentence is weaker than the neighbours and far stronger than chance.
- **NEU-175 — the direction is omitted, and it is the opposite of what a reader
  will assume.** The retained introns are **predominantly decreasing in
  retention**, and the two introns in the regulator's own transcript are **less
  retained**. **And it is two introns, not one.**
- **NEU-176 — the significant result is a proportion, not an amount.** The cited
  line is the abstract. The body says **total message did not increase**, that the
  nuclear and cytoplasmic changes **were not significant**, and that what was
  significant is the **proportion** in the cytoplasm, **in two of three genotype
  groups.**
- **NEU-141 — a threshold the paper does not claim.** The source reports two
  detection points and **says the opposite for its other series**, where cryptic
  exon expression correlated with the degree of knockdown, and attributes the
  floor to decay. The caveat **survives only in the note.**
- **NEU-153 — "only" excludes a reduction the paper reports.** The source
  reports the risk allele giving **a minor reduction in affinity** as well.
- **NEU-179 — scope too wide, twice.** The knockout null is **in a non-neuronal
  line**, and **both nulls are reanalyses of other groups' datasets** rather than
  experiments in that paper. The record states neither.
- **NEU-161 — proposal as finding.** The 3 per cent in-frame fraction is **an
  introduction sentence restating two earlier studies**, and a sibling source in
  the same block gives a different figure for mouse. The note says so; **the claim,
  the only citable field, does not.**

### Two numbers to fix
- **NEU-164's denominator**: the plasma assay cohort is 66 of 43 carriers, but
  **the correlation was run on 58 plus 30**.
- **NEU-155's note**: "three variants against two" is wrong. **The other paper
  built the repeat construct too**, so "neither paper tests the other's
  configuration" fails on that dimension. The cell system, backbone and readout do
  differ.

### Mechanical
- **Two author strings have multi-word surnames collapsed into initials**, which
  is the same class of defect as the earlier lowercase-diacritic case: a real name
  mangled by reformatting. Three other multi-word surnames in the same block were
  handled correctly.
- **One year conflict**, record against corpus and against its own bibkey; an
  advance-publication issue year is possible, so it cannot be adjudicated here.
- **Six tier strings say "correlation of isoform ratio with a phenotype" where no
  phenotype is measured** (a pipeline concordance, a restated fraction, a
  cross-species overlap). They do not overstate; a descriptive tier is right.
- **Two notes say no denominator or no effect size is available. True of the
  abstract line only** — both documents have full text carrying the cohorts.

### Confirmed, and worth the chapter's weight
- **Item 1**: decay inhibition raised two cryptic events and left the third
  unchanged, and the weakest knockdown revealed one only under inhibition. **So
  the 50 per cent figure is the assay's floor**, exactly as the chapter needed.
- **Item 3**: every denominator checked and correct, including 11 of 95 shared
  between two datasets from one cell line and 12 of 26 recovered from an earlier
  report, and 66 of 221 in two or more of three cell types with 32 in all three.
- **Item 4**: **no sensitivity or specificity exists anywhere in that paper**,
  which defers both to future work, and the limit of detection **sits above the
  positivity threshold.** The control group with the second-highest concentrations
  is non-significant.
- **Item 6**: confirmed in full, every cohort, interval and survival figure.
- **The personal-communication item is clean.** A rescue result cited only as a
  personal communication appears in exactly one note, which correctly states it is
  not recorded, and **no record asserts it.**
- **Three author clusters confirmed name by name**, and two corrections to them:
  one record the notes treat as separate **links to all three clusters**, and one
  pair the notes treat as possibly related **is genuinely independent**, the only
  surname match across 99 and 35 names being two different people.

## Block NEU-222..259 (25 ok, 13 with defects). One arithmetic error, and it is a denominator.

### An independence note that is exactly inverted
**Third time today.** A note says the retinal results' independence "could not be
checked, because the author lists are not in the corpus". **The lists are in the
records.** Checking them:
- **Two of the records share two authors**, so **the mouse result and the
  no-phenotype result are not independent of each other** — and the note that
  calls them "in tension" does not say so.
- **A third shares a senior author with both**, and **its note is silent.**
- **The fourth shares no author with any of the three**, so **the organoid result
  is the independent leg — the opposite of what its own note implies.**

### The arithmetic error, and it is the kind that survives every gate
**NEU-245 builds a percentage on the wrong set.** The 24,445 variants across
2,100 genes are the extended-acceptor set. **The 11.7 per cent is a fraction of
the pathogenic variants in a different set** — 5,463 pathogenic of 30,042 — and
**that denominator is on two lines the record does not cite.** Worse, the claim
as written is near-vacuous: **within the extended-acceptor set essentially every
pathogenic variant is outside the essential dinucleotide by definition.**

### A direction inverted, in both the claim and the note
**NEU-251.** The source says each of two database figures is **closer to our 27
per cent than to the other database's 17 per cent.** The record turns this into a
comparison of **each figure's distance from 27 against the third figure's
distance from 27** — and in that form **it is false for one of the two**, since
43 is 16 away from 27 while 17 is only 10 away. **Both the claim and the note
carry the inversion.** The claim also drops the same line's characterisations:
one figure "consistent with our data", the other "above our upper bound".

### Two records take the limitation half of a passage that defends the method
**NEU-244 and NEU-253.** Both quote verbatim limitations of an assay. **Both omit
the sentence the same line closes on**, where the authors say the assay is
generally well correlated with patient material, has a sufficient track record to
be accepted as functional evidence, and **may still correctly flag a variant even
when it gets the outcome wrong.** One record drops "definitively" too.
**Another record in the same range handles the identical situation correctly**,
which is what makes these defects rather than style.

### A true finding omitted on a false premise
**NEU-246's note** says a sentence is truncated in the corpus text so a
sensitivity-specificity pair could not be read together. **The whole sentence is
there**, and a figure legend repeats both numbers with both sample sizes. **A real
result was left out because of a wrong belief about the corpus.**

### Four tier strings that assert what their own notes deny
**NEU-233, NEU-235, NEU-236, NEU-237.** Each tier claims an isoform-specific
perturbation or an isoform-ratio correlation, and **each record's own note says
in the next breath that the criterion is not met** — the perturbation is of a
splicing factor's gene dose, or the ratio is inferred from a tract length and
never measured. **NEU-228 is the same**, and its note concedes "the isoform ratio
is not measured here at all".

### Scope and hedge losses
- **NEU-241**: an 89 per cent concordance figure is **measured on seven named
  cancer-predisposition genes** and presented as general. Neither claim nor note
  says so.
- **NEU-227**: five retention percentages with a pooled sample size of 13, where
  **two of the five points rest on one and two subjects.** The per-genotype counts
  are in the note only, so a five-point stepwise curve can be quoted with no sign
  of that.
- **NEU-230**: the claim contrasts two odds ratios where **the authors state the
  two are statistically indistinguishable, p = 0.56.** That p-value is in neither
  field.
- **NEU-223**: the source's own "additional samples would be needed to determine
  statistical significance" survives **only in the note**, on n = 1 against n = 1.
- **NEU-225**: an exception is dropped entirely — one muscle in one sex at one age
  **did not reach significance** — and "at most tested age timepoints" becomes "at
  every age tested".
- **NEU-259 is the least citable fraction in the range**: two percentages with no
  numerator, no denominator and **no assay named**, in a section whose whole
  argument is that a fraction means nothing without its ascertainment. **The
  source names the assay on the cited line.**

### Dead cross-references
**Two records point at an id removed from the file today**, one of them in its
claim, where "the same variant" now has no antecedent. The independence statements
remain true of the replacement record but name a thing that is gone.

### Mechanical
- **Seven of 38 records carry no tier line at all** — not one of the three
  strings, not a descriptive tier, not "no tier applies". Absence is a different
  gap from a mismatch.
- **Five notes still say a field needs resolving from the DOI when the record
  already carries it**, correctly, from Crossref. Stale rather than wrong.
- **Four papers each carry four or five records, and most of those records declare
  no shared-source relationship.**
- **One paper is internally inconsistent about an exon-length cutoff**, "at most
  100" in one place and "fewer than 100" in another. Two records are each faithful
  to their own line, **and a chapter printing both will look wrong.**
- **One dispatch premise of mine could not be confirmed**: the record I pointed at
  scopes only one of the two figures I described, and the other source **states no
  exon-length restriction and no access date at all.** So the correction exists for
  one figure, not two.

## Block NEU-181..219 (28 ok, 11 with defects). Every figure exact; every quote byte-exact.

### A new defect class: notes that assert absence, where the thing is present
**Five notes in this block say information is unavailable. In four cases it is
available in a document the block already cites.**
- **One note says an approval date "appears in no document read here". It appears
  twice**, in a document two other records in the block cite — and **a second FDA
  document gives a different year**, so the chapter must pick one or report both.
- **One note says the arm sizes "must not be stated". The same review gives
  them**, for the same analysis, in a document already cited.
- **Two notes say authorship cannot be established because the corpus metadata
  carries no author list. Both records' own source blocks settle it.** One pair
  turns out to be **independent**, which strengthens a contradiction the chapter
  records. **The other pair shares two authors and is one laboratory, so an
  off-target result is not an independent test of the discovery paper's
  oligonucleotide** — and the note implies it is.
- **One note says an approval date is absent from the corpus. It is present**, in
  a different drug's European assessment restating it, which is worth attributing
  as such rather than to the drug's own file.

**This is the mirror image of the independence problem**: a note that asserts
absence stops anyone looking, and it is wrong as often as a note that asserts
independence.

### Two records are the same trial and neither says so
**One record is a trial's interim biopsy analysis and another is its final
topline, and they are the same study** — the identifier in one document is named
as the trial in another. **The chapter would read them as two trials.** The
confirmatory designation is also better anchored than the record's note claims,
in two documents.

### A number set that is not what the record thinks
**NEU-187: there are three sets, not two.** The review's headline responder
figures are **the sensitivity analysis including patients who died or withdrew**,
not the prespecified interim set, which has different denominators. **And the
publication's own interim differs from the review's headline too.** The record's
note spots one mismatch and misses the other. The same interim-against-final trap
appears in a second record, where the responder comparator is 21 per cent in the
review and 26 in the publication, **and that is recorded nowhere.**

### An inference attributed to the source
**NEU-218.** The claim says the therapeutic range came from transgenic mice.
**The document gives the mouse range as 1 to 10 and the therapeutic range as 5 to
10, and never says where the second came from.** Attributing it to mice is the
record's inference, repeated in its note. Also, **the line reports a drug
concentration only** — no exon inclusion was measured in human tissue, so "target
engagement was checked" overstates.

### A rank order that the reviewer says is not a dose effect
**NEU-208's note calls two medians an "opposite rank order between doses". The
same review states there was no dose-related difference.** The
assay-dependence point stands, roughly twofold between two methods on the same
samples, **and the rank-order point must not be presented as a dose effect.**
The record also omits a third assay, and the normalisation that the headline
medians depend on — **a different normalisation in the same review gives entirely
different numbers.**

### Attribution dropped where the block's own argument depends on it
**NEU-210** drops "that he interprets as showing" from one official's conclusion
that higher protein was not associated with better function. **That matters
because the paired record has another official offering calculations in the
opposite direction.** One record attributes properly and the other does not.

**And the opposition was larger than the chapter can currently say: three named
officials opposed that approval**, one of whom would have declined it outright,
against one in favour, with the Commissioner deferring. **That count lives only
in a note.**

### Scope and proposal defects
- **NEU-196** calls a therapy approved and its trial the first of its approach.
  **The source says neither**, names no drug, and gives a registration the claim
  does not carry. **No document in the block states an approval type, date or
  basis for it.**
- **NEU-198** states a generalised mechanism as what four compounds do. **The
  source says only that they contact the epitope similarly, "suggesting" the
  mechanism generalises.** The note attributes it correctly; the claim does not.
- **NEU-219's lead** says the limit is distribution rather than chemistry. **The
  source makes no such comparison**, and the record's own note says the chemistry
  differs from the approved drugs, so **the lead is in tension with its own note.**
- **NEU-214's author field is a garbled initialism** that will not build a
  bibliography entry, and its claim drops "unadjusted" while omitting the adjusted
  figure, the timepoint and the denominator.

### Five tier strings claim an organismal phenotype where only a protein was measured
**NEU-199, NEU-204, NEU-205, NEU-207, NEU-209**, and the same judgement applies to
two more. In every one the measured outcome is **a protein quantity in muscle and
nothing else** — and in one case the same review records **a poor correlation with
walking distance, R = 0.14, with the slope not positive.** The file already
qualifies this tier elsewhere, so the convention exists.

### Twelve claims state a trial result without an arm, a denominator or a timepoint
**Six of the twelve could have carried it**, from lines in documents the block
already cites: a trial name twice, per-arm sizes, an assay name, a normalisation,
and a timepoint. **The other six cannot: the source gives none.** The worst is a
load-bearing figure with **no denominator at all in the claim**, its sample sizes
living in a neighbouring record and in its own note.

### One internal ambiguity the chapter must resolve explicitly
One document uses the same 0.93 per cent as both **an increase** and **a level**,
in different places. The record follows one reading without citing the line that
supports it. **A chapter sentence has to say which.**

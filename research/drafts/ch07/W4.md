## Deep-intronic variants and pseudoexons

[NEU-222] @foley2024col6a1intron11 (2024) conf=high
  The international cohort assembled for the COL6A1 intron 11 variant reached 44 patients, and the paper breaks the ascertainment down explicitly: 4 were found by muscle RNA sequencing in patients negative for a causative variant in COL6A1, COL6A2 and COL6A3, 27 by targeted Sanger sequencing of that intron in further such patients, and 13 only after the variant was added to diagnostic sequencing panels.

[NEU-223] @foley2024col6a1intron11 (2024) conf=medium
  One patient in that cohort was somatically mosaic for the COL6A1 intron 11 variant, carrying the variant allele at a fractional abundance of about 20 per cent by droplet digital PCR, which the authors read as roughly 40 per cent of cells, and in a fresh skin biopsy the pseudoexon-containing transcripts were 7.2-fold lower than in a heterozygous patient, alongside a milder phenotype the authors call consistent with Bethlem muscular dystrophy rather than Ullrich. The paper states that additional samples would be needed to determine whether the 7.2-fold difference is statistically significant.

[NEU-224] @bolduc2024col6a1mouse (2024) conf=high
  The mechanism of the COL6A1 intron 11 variant is that c.930+189C>T creates a splice donor which, with a cryptic acceptor upstream of it, inserts an in-frame 72-nucleotide pseudoexon into the mature transcript. A humanized knock-in mouse carrying that human sequence includes the pseudoexon in 7.6 to 10.6 per cent of transcripts when heterozygous and 15.0 to 20.8 per cent when homozygous, across quadriceps, tibialis anterior, gastrocnemius and diaphragm, against a mean of 24.7 per cent in biopsies from three heterozygous patients, so only the homozygous mouse reproduces the human inclusion level.

[NEU-225] @bolduc2024col6a1mouse (2024) conf=high
  In that mouse the humanized allele alone caused no phenotype relative to wild type, while the alleles carrying the pseudoexon-creating variant gave weaker forelimb grip strength at most ages tested between 4 and 20 weeks in both heterozygotes and homozygotes, with one muscle in one sex at one age not reaching significance; tibialis anterior and gastrocnemius masses at 8 and 24 weeks were significantly lower in homozygotes but in heterozygotes the decrease did not consistently reach significance, and body weights did not differ between any genotypes. That the humanized allele alone is silent is what attributes the phenotype to the splicing change rather than to the humanization.

[NEU-226] @pitsava2024pseudoexons (2024) conf=high
  Short-read genome sequencing of 353 families drawn from one research study of suspected Mendelian conditions with prior inconclusive clinical genetic testing plus two cardiac cohorts whose members were undiagnosed but had not had harmonised prior testing produced a molecular diagnosis in 54 families, 15.3 per cent of the families sequenced, and 5 of those 54 diagnoses were deep-intronic variants, every one of which was shown by RNA sequencing to create a pseudoexon and every one of which had an inconclusive SpliceAI score.
  FIGURE: Two bars for the same five patients, one over the 54 solved families and one over the 353 sequenced families, to show that one numerator yields two defensible fractions.

## Massively parallel splicing assays and the size of the class

[NEU-239] @multiplexedexonrecognition2019 (2019) conf=high  [ABSTRACT ONLY]
  Run over population rather than disease variation, a sort-based multiplexed splicing reporter assayed 27,733 Exome Aggregation Consortium variants in and around 2,198 human exons and found 3.8 per cent of them, 1,050 variants, to be large-effect splice disruptors, with 83 per cent of those lying outside the canonical splice sites.
  FIGURE: One axis of splice-disruption rate with a point per assay, each point labelled with where its variants came from - population database, publication-derived disease database, trio de novo set, clinical submissions - so that the ordering reads as ascertainment.

[NEU-240] @canson2026constructassays (2026) conf=high
  An audit of roughly 41,000 variants from 21 published construct-based splicing datasets across 5,458 genes found that the scores produced by five of the seven massively parallel splicing assays reviewed could not separate aberrant from natural splicing events, and that the datasets generally did not report which transcript a variant induced.

[NEU-241] @canson2026constructassays (2026) conf=high
  The same audit put reporter measurements against measurements in patient tissue and found them to agree more often than not but not always: for 91 variants in seven named cancer-predisposition genes with both kinds of data, minigene and patient-derived RNA results showed complete or high concordance in the predominant transcript in 89 per cent of cases, 81 of 91.

[NEU-242] @smith2023splicebenchmark (2023) conf=high
  Benchmarked against saturation splicing assays on 3,616 variants in five genes, eight splice prediction tools agreed with the measurements only modestly, with a median Pearson correlation of 0.53 and a range from minus 0.06 to 0.85, and they agreed with each other no better, median pairwise correlation 0.58 across a range from 0.04 to 0.97, with agreement worse inside exons than in introns.

[NEU-243] @smith2023splicebenchmark (2023) conf=high
  Part of the disagreement is annotation rather than biology: in that benchmark, including or excluding an alternative splice form from the gene model changed SpliceAI's prediction for more than 10 per cent of variants at two of the assayed exons, and across GTEx junction data the authors estimate that 18.0 per cent of protein-coding genes, 3,571 of 19,817, carry an alternative splice site used at 20 per cent or more in at least one tissue that is absent from SpliceAI's default annotation.

[NEU-244] @smith2023splicebenchmark (2023) conf=high
  The systematic gap between a reporter and the real locus is stated by the benchmark's own authors: the assays they used excluded deep-intronic variants, which they call an important source of pathogenic splice disruption, because the lengths involved are hard to screen, and plasmid-borne assays cannot register effects that come from transcription elongation rate or nucleosome positioning. The same passage closes by defending the assay, saying it is generally well correlated with patient material, has a sufficient track record to be accepted as functional evidence, and may still correctly flag a variant even where it gets the outcome wrong.

[NEU-249] @canson2026constructassays (2026) conf=high
  Of the seven construct-based massively parallel splicing datasets audited - three built on the massively parallel splicing assay, one of them the 2017 study behind this chapter's roughly 10 per cent figure, together with Vex-seq, a sort-based exon-recognition assay, a calibrated gene-specific minigene assay and a TTN assay - only two produced score distributions that were multimodal on Hartigan's dip test at p below 2 times ten to the minus sixteen and separated by the thresholds their own studies used, and the three massively parallel splicing assay datasets were not among the two.

[NEU-253] @lord2019splicesites (2019) conf=high
  The same paper tested a handful of its candidate variants in a minigene and got a mixed result: splicing changed for five of six likely diagnostic polypyrimidine tract variants and for the likely diagnostic donor plus 5 variant, but not for one further likely diagnostic polypyrimidine variant nor for one of uncertain significance, and the authors say they cannot definitively state that what a minigene shows would hold in the patient's full genetic, developmental and cellular context. The same passage closes by saying the assay is generally well correlated with patient material and has a sufficient track record to be accepted as functional evidence.

[NEU-255] @soemedi2017mapsy (2017) conf=high
  The same assay was checked against patient material: of 32 exonic splicing mutations it had called, 26, about 81 per cent, were confirmed in RNA from patient lymphoblastoid cell lines, fibroblasts, whole blood or post-mortem brain, with the samples chosen solely on availability and some confirmations taken from published follow-up studies rather than generated afresh.

[NEU-256] @soemedi2017mapsy (2017) conf=high
  The same assay's own stated reach is half the problem: only exons shorter than 100 nucleotides could be tested, and with the average internal human exon around 130 nucleotides the authors say half of human exons are inaccessible to it, while flanking splice sites, differing transcription efficiencies and tissue-specific effects are not preserved in the construct at all.

[NEU-258] @canson2026constructassays (2026) conf=high
  Where three multiplexed assays can be compared on the same variant class they do not agree. Among variants at the canonical donor and acceptor dinucleotides, where splicing disruption is the expected outcome for nearly all of them, a gene-specific assay called 97 per cent spliceogenic, 30 of 31, its single exception also predicted neutral by SpliceAI, while a sort-based library assay called 83 per cent, 180 of 217, and a third called only 76 per cent, 16 of 21.
  FIGURE: Three assays side by side on the same positional classes - canonical dinucleotides, splice region, elsewhere - with the proportion each called disruptive, so the reader sees that the disagreement is largest where it should be smallest.

[NEU-259] @canson2026constructassays (2026) conf=high
  The same audit measured each assay's calls against a common predictor and found them far apart: 99 per cent of the variants one assay reported as abnormally spliced carried a SpliceAI score of 0.2 or more, where for the other massively parallel assays that proportion ran from 9 to 66 per cent, each proportion taken over the variants that assay called abnormally spliced.

## Splice-gain against splice-loss

[NEU-245] @bryen2022agexclusion (2022) conf=high
  Of the 24,445 variants in the extended acceptor region reported to ClinVar and the Leiden Open Variation Database across 2,100 genes, 638 pathogenic variants lay outside the essential AG, and within that subset creation was the largest single category: 41.9 per cent created a new AG dinucleotide in the polypyrimidine tract, against 28.2 per cent that removed the pyrimidine at position minus 3 and 16.6 per cent that removed one or more pyrimidines.
  FIGURE: A stacked bar of the acceptor-region pathogenic mechanisms, with the created-AG segment split by distance from the branchpoint, so the high-risk window is visible as a position effect rather than a rule.

[NEU-246] @bryen2022agexclusion (2022) conf=high
  Where a created AG falls decides whether it matters. Of 1,077 AG-creating variants in the acceptor region, 262 were classified pathogenic and 198 benign, with 496 uncertain and 121 conflicting, and 93.1 per cent of the pathogenic ones lay in a window more than 6 nucleotides downstream of the predicted branchpoint and more than 5 upstream of the acceptor against 44.9 per cent of the benign ones - a window from which naturally occurring AG dinucleotides are correspondingly depleted, only 5.8 per cent of them falling in it.

## Synonymous, intronic and modifier variants acting through splicing

[NEU-227] @sterrantino2021cftrtgtn (2021) conf=medium
  Measured directly in nasal brushings from 13 subjects homozygous for one CFTR intron 9 (TG)mTn haplotype, the proportion of CFTR mRNA that retains exon 10 falls stepwise with tract composition: 94.7 per cent for (TG)10T9 from three subjects, 88.0 per cent for (TG)10T7 from two, 80.9 per cent for (TG)11T7 from five, 47.5 per cent for (TG)11T5 from one and 27.6 per cent for (TG)12T5 from two, so the same tract produces a continuous range of residual correct splicing rather than two classes, on groups small enough that two of the five points rest on one and two subjects.

[NEU-228] @shteinberg2017arg117his (2017) conf=high
  Among 62 cystic fibrosis patients carrying p.Arg117His in Northern Ireland, 42 had a 5T intron 9 tract and 13 a 7T tract, and the group with 5T and a class I or II mutation in trans was the more severely affected: in the clinical table that group holds 39 patients against 10 with 7T, 6 with p.Gly551Asp and 191 p.Phe508del homozygotes, with mean sweat chloride 82 plus or minus 14 mEq per litre in the 28 of them measured against 35 plus or minus 8 in the 10 measured with 7T, and modelled annual decline in FEV1 per cent predicted of minus 0.6 per cent against plus 0.17 per cent for 7T and minus 1.02 per cent for the p.Phe508del homozygotes.

[NEU-229] @shteinberg2017arg117his (2017) conf=high
  The bound on that same comparison comes from the same paper: once the four genotype groups were tested against each other, only the 5T group against p.Arg117His/p.Gly551Asp and p.Arg117His/p.Gly551Asp against p.Phe508del homozygotes differed significantly in rate of FEV1 decline, and the 5T against 7T difference did not reach statistical significance.

[NEU-230] @nykamp2020polyt (2020) conf=high
  In 548,300 individuals sequenced at one clinical laboratory, the CFTR T5 allele had a minor allele frequency of 4.2 per cent, and when paired with a cystic fibrosis-causing variant the three T5 haplotypes were enriched to graded degrees among people whose requisition reported a personal or family history of cystic fibrosis or a CFTR-related disorder, relative to people referred for hereditary cancer testing who had not requested CFTR analysis: odds ratio 6.9 for TG11T5, 32.0 for TG12T5 and 78.8 for TG13T5, with TG13T5 homozygotes at 255.7 against 581.7 for carriers of two disease-causing variants, a difference the authors report as not statistically distinguishable at p = 0.56, and with carriers of p.Arg117His excluded from the analysis.
  FIGURE: The T5-containing genotypes on one axis ordered by measured exon 10 retention from NEU-227, with the enrichment odds ratio on the other, to show whether splicing level predicts clinical penetrance monotonically.

[NEU-231] @sheikh2013mecp2synonymous (2013) conf=high
  A de novo synonymous MECP2 variant, c.48C>T, in a girl with Rett syndrome creates a splice donor inside exon 1 that removes the last 16 nucleotides of that exon, and because translation of the E1 isoform starts in exon 1 while in the E2 isoform the same stretch lies in the 5' untranslated region, the deletion frameshifts only E1; a 5' nuclease assay on patient lymphocyte RNA found the correct E1 transcript reduced about twofold and the E2 transcript raised about 1.4-fold against three healthy controls.

[NEU-238] @kaltak2023abca4splicing (2023) conf=high
  Missense variants make up about half the causal alleles in Stargardt disease, and when 18 synonymous or missense ABCA4 variants drawn from 5,579 biallelic Stargardt probands - 15 of them picked because SpliceAI predicted a new acceptor or donor above a delta score of 0.20 with no prior report of a splicing effect, and 3 with scores below 0.20 - were tested in midigenes carrying 4 to 12 kilobases of genomic sequence, 16 of the 18 produced splice aberrations, so for those variants a splicing effect is demonstrated alongside whatever the amino-acid change does.

## What fraction of pathogenic variants act through splicing

[NEU-025] @mort2014mutpredsplice (2014) conf=medium
  A machine-learning estimate over exonic variants put the splice-disrupting fraction at approximately 16 per cent of inherited disease exonic mutations and approximately 10 to 14 per cent of cancer exonic mutations, a predicted rather than assayed share and of exonic variants only. The authors add that the cancer set contains a large proportion of passenger variants, which they say will almost certainly make their cancer figure a serious under-estimate of the splicing-sensitive share of cancer driver mutations.
  FIGURE: A single figure placing each published estimate on a common axis with its denominator labelled - exonic HGMD variants, all disease alleles, splice-region variants, pathogenic variants in rare disease - so the spread reads as different questions rather than disagreement about one number.

[NEU-026] @soemedi2017mapsy (2017) conf=high
  A massively parallel splicing assay of 4,964 exonic disease alleles drawn from HGMD found that about 10 per cent of them (513) disrupted splicing both in cells and in vitro, against 3 per cent of the common SNPs tested the same way.

[NEU-027] @soemedi2017mapsy (2017) conf=medium
  The one-third figure - that one third of disease alleles alter splicing - enters this literature as the authors' summary of previous studies rather than as a measurement they report, so it should not be counted as an independent estimate.

[NEU-028] @jaganathan2019spliceai (2019) conf=high  [ABSTRACT ONLY]
  The SpliceAI authors estimated that cryptic splicing causes 9 to 11 per cent of the pathogenic mutations carried by patients who have rare genetic disorders, and they describe cryptic splicing as a class of disease variation previously underappreciated.

[NEU-029] @lord2019splicesites (2019) conf=high
  Within splice-region variants specifically, a de novo mutation burden analysis across 7,833 developmental disorder probands and their parents apportioned 73 per cent of the pathogenic contribution to the canonical dinucleotides and 27 per cent to flanking non-canonical positions.

[NEU-030] @lord2019splicesites (2019) conf=high
  Published estimates of the non-canonical share disagree by more than twofold, and the same paper's discussion sets its own trio-based 27 per cent against roughly 30 per cent from HGMD, roughly 43 per cent from another curation and roughly 17 per cent in ClinVar, attributing the spread to how each set's variants were ascertained.
  FIGURE: A dot plot of the four non-canonical-share estimates with each one annotated by how its variants were ascertained - population constraint, publication-derived database, curated set, clinical submissions - showing that ascertainment, not biology, orders them.

[NEU-254] @soemedi2017mapsy (2017) conf=high
  The denominator behind the massively parallel assay's roughly 10 per cent is narrower than "exonic disease alleles" suggests: the 4,964 variants were non-synonymous mutations classified disease-causing in HGMD as accessed in May 2012, restricted to internal exons of 100 nucleotides or fewer that fitted a 170-nucleotide genomic window, so synonymous variants are absent from the denominator altogether and the figure is a rate among non-synonymous variants in short exons.

[NEU-257] @soemedi2017mapsy (2017) conf=high
  The one-third figure is not only a summary of earlier work but a prediction, and not an independent voice in the debate: the introduction of the massively parallel assay paper gives it as the share of inherited disease alleles that an algorithm expects to mis-splice to some degree, citing one 2011 study whose last author is also the last author of the assay paper itself.

## Why the published fractions do not converge

[NEU-247] @sharo2023clinvarhgmd (2023) conf=high
  The two databases behind the published fractions do not classify to the same standard. Taking inborn errors of metabolism screened in newborns as a test case, where a pathogenic genotype found in a healthy adult is almost certainly an error of classification, the set HGMD marks disease-causing points to roughly a hundredfold more sick people among 1000 Genomes participants than the set ClinVar marks pathogenic or likely pathogenic does.

[NEU-248] @sharo2023clinvarhgmd (2023) conf=high
  The reason the two databases are not comparable is structural. ClinVar takes more than 93 per cent of its content from clinical laboratories, which usually classify by the standardised guidelines, and it lists benign and uncertain variants as well as pathogenic ones; HGMD is compiled from published papers and keeps no entry for a variant called benign or uncertain, so a fraction computed over HGMD has no benign denominator available to it.

[NEU-250] @lord2019splicesites (2019) conf=high
  The de novo burden analysis compared its own estimate against a clinical database on matched positions and found them incompatible: against its 27 per cent for non-canonical positions, with a 95 per cent confidence interval of 18 to 39 per cent, ClinVar pathogenic and likely pathogenic variants overlapping the same positions with non-synonymous consequences removed put 83.5 per cent in canonical positions and only 16.5 per cent in non-canonical ones, or 17.5 per cent after adjusting for submission count, both significantly lower at Fisher's exact p of 1.22 times ten to the minus fifteen and below 2.2 times ten to the minus sixteen, which those authors read as clinical databases under-ascertaining non-canonical splicing variants by about 35 to 40 per cent.
  FIGURE: One axis of non-canonical share, with the trio-based estimate shown as a point with its 18 to 39 per cent interval and each database-derived estimate as a point beside it, so the reader sees that two of them sit outside the interval rather than near it.

[NEU-251] @lord2019splicesites (2019) conf=high
  The same paper gives the general reason the older figures cannot be read at face value, that estimates of this split are sparse and typically rest on clinically interpreted variants and so inherit whatever biases current clinical practice has, and then declines to use the scope differences to dismiss them: although the HGMD-derived roughly 30 per cent and the roughly 43 per cent from a different curation cover recessive as well as dominant disease and include exonic variants, which its own de novo analysis of dominant disorders does not, those authors say each of the two sits closer to their own 27 per cent than to the ClinVar figure of about 17 per cent, calling one consistent with their data and the other above their upper bound.

[NEU-252] @lord2019splicesites (2019) conf=high
  That 27 per cent is a lower bound by its authors' own account, because the analysis left out exonic missense variants, branchpoints, splicing enhancers and silencers, and deep-intronic mutations, which means the 35 to 40 per cent under-ascertainment they infer for clinical databases may itself be conservative.

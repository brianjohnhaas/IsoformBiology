## SCN1A poison exon 20N as a disease mechanism

[NEU-341] @poisonexon2018dravetscn1a (2018) conf=high  [ABSTRACT ONLY]
  Seven likely pathogenic variants were reported outside the annotated coding exons of SCN1A, the gene most often implicated in epilepsy, and five of them were shown to push a non-coding "poison" exon into the transcript, lowering the amount of full-length SCN1A protein; that put a naturally occurring non-productive exon forward as a disease mechanism rather than only as a curiosity of annotation.

## a platform limit from species conservation

[NEU-371] @venkatesh2024stk002 (2024) conf=high
  The approach can fail on the species it needs for testing: neither mouse nor rat carries the OPA1 non-productive exon, because the 3-prime splice site is not conserved, so rodents could not be used for proof of mechanism in vivo and a separate rabbit-specific surrogate oligonucleotide had to be built to run the experiment at all.

## a poison-exon variant modelled in mouse

[NEU-346] @voskobiynyk2021poisonexon (2021) conf=high
  A group with no connection to the sponsor built the human lesion into a mouse and measured what the exon normally costs: heterozygous knock-in mice carrying the orthologue of a patient intron 20 variant lost about half their brain Scn1a mRNA and Nav1.1 protein and showed premature mortality, seizures and hyperactivity, while quantitative RT-PCR put exon 20N inclusion at about 1 per cent of Scn1a mRNA in adult wild-type brain and about five-fold higher in the knock-in.

## a regulator's hold on redosing, and its release

[NEU-388] @isrctn12811235longwing (2026) conf=medium
  Repeat dosing in the extension was gated by the regulator rather than by the protocol alone: the LONGWING record states that dosing beyond the initial dose on day 1 would not occur until the Medicines and Healthcare products Regulatory Agency approved the study to proceed, with the second and third doses at week 16 and week 32 each conditional on that approval, and notes that the agency subsequently granted it for the second, third and additional doses.

## a test of disease modification the trials do not run

[NEU-378] @yuji2026scbtolerance (2026) conf=medium
  Two commentators argue that the programme has built in a blind spot: sodium channel blockers are contraindicated in Dravet syndrome because they further impair the interneurons the disease already weakens, so whether restored NaV1.1 removes that contraindication would be the strongest available test of disease modification, and both the extension studies and the phase 3 EMPEROR trial continue to exclude patients on maintenance sodium channel blockers, leaving the test unmade.

## adverse events in the zorevunersen programme

[NEU-357] @zorevunersen2026nejm (2026) conf=high  [ABSTRACT ONLY]
  The safety profile is dominated by the route and by one laboratory finding: most adverse events were mild or moderate, the commonest being post-lumbar-puncture syndrome in 25 per cent of patients in the phase 1 to 2a studies and an elevated cerebrospinal fluid protein level in 45 per cent in the extension studies, and across the programme one patient had suspected unexpected serious adverse reactions, one had an adverse event leading to withdrawal, two died of sudden unexpected death in epilepsy and one died of malnutrition.

## allele-specific correction of a poison-exon variant

[NEU-374] @tang2025scn1apoisonexons (2025) conf=high
  In neurons from a patient carrying a variant inside the intron 1 poison exon, fewer than 1 per cent of targeted long-read sequencing reads carried the poison exon in each of three control lines against an average sixteen-fold rise to between 3 and 6 per cent in the patient's neurons, and an exon-skipping oligonucleotide overlapping the variant both reduced that inclusion and significantly reduced the ratio of variant-allele to wild-type-allele poison-exon reads, so it acted with substantial allele specificity despite differing from the wild-type sequence at a single nucleotide.

## an independent group raising a haploinsufficient gene by the same route

[NEU-370] @dawickimckenna2023syngap1 (2023) conf=high
  An unconnected group reached the same strategy for a different neurodevelopmental gene: mapping where the neuronal splicing factor PTBP2 binds in human brain identified a non-productive alternative 3-prime splice site in SYNGAP1 exon 11x, oligonucleotide walks across it raised productive splicing, SYNGAP1 mRNA and SYNGAP1 protein dose-dependently in HEK293T cells, and in neurons differentiated from two patients' induced pluripotent stem cells carrying the nonsense variants R1240X and K1185X, which showed a 50 per cent reduction in SYNGAP1 mRNA, the oligonucleotides raised SYNGAP1 expression.

## conservation of the SCN1A poison exon

[NEU-343] @lim2020tango (2020) conf=high
  The SCN1A poison exon is old enough to be shared with mice: the non-coding exon and its flanking intronic sequence are 96 per cent conserved between human and mouse by conservation score, human exon 20x corresponding to mouse exon 21x, and about 16 per cent of non-productive exon-inclusion events across the genome were found to be as conserved as coding exons.

## delivery and target engagement

[NEU-218] @fdanusinersenclinpharm2016 (2016) conf=high
  A splicing drug's concentration was measured in the tissue it is meant to act on: spinal cord from three patients who died during the phase 2 nusinersen study CS3A carried more than 11 micrograms of drug per gram, with a range of 11.9 to 31.8, at doses of 6 to 12 milligrams, above the 5 to 10 micrograms per gram the reviewers give as the targeted therapeutic range in central nervous system tissue. The same review separately gives 1 to 10 micrograms per gram as the concentration producing 50 to 90 per cent SMN2 exon 7 inclusion in transgenic mice.
  FIGURE: A concentration axis with the mouse dose-response band for exon 7 inclusion, the range the reviewers adopted as therapeutic, and the three measured human spinal cord values - so the reader sees how a human dose is justified from a mouse curve.

[NEU-219] @heemskerk2010asopk (2010) conf=high
  An oligonucleotide's delivery to muscle is uneven between muscle groups and between healthy and diseased fibres: in mdx mice, 2-prime-O-methyl phosphorothioate oligonucleotide levels and half-life differed between muscle groups, with heart showing the lowest levels but the longest half-life at about 46 days, and levels were higher in dystrophin-deficient fibres than in healthy ones, which produced correspondingly higher exon skipping in the diseased muscle.

## delivery to the central nervous system as an exclusion criterion

[NEU-386] @isrctn99651026admiral (2026) conf=high
  Two exclusions in the UK registrations are about the needle rather than the disease: both ADMIRAL and its extension exclude any patient in whom a spinal deformity, or another condition, may alter how freely cerebrospinal fluid moves, or who has an implanted cerebrospinal fluid drainage shunt, alongside excluding maintenance treatment with an antiepileptic drug acting primarily as a sodium channel blocker, named as phenytoin, carbamazepine, oxcarbazepine, lamotrigine, lacosamide or rufinamide.

## developmental regulation of the SCN1A poison exon

[NEU-347] @voskobiynyk2021poisonexon (2021) conf=high
  The exon looks like a developmental dimmer switch rather than an error: in a public RNA-seq series of mouse cortex, about 70 per cent of Scn1a transcripts carried exon 20N at embryonic day 14.5, falling gradually to under 10 per cent by postnatal day 30 and staying minimal thereafter, with total Scn1a mRNA rising as inclusion fell, and the homologous Scn8a poison exon 18N behaved the same way.

## further poison exons in SCN1A as additional drug targets

[NEU-372] @tang2025scn1apoisonexons (2025) conf=high
  SCN1A turns out to carry three poison exons, not one: targeted deep-coverage long-read sequencing of SCN1A transcripts in neurons derived from healthy control stem cells found rare but consistent inclusion of two further frame-disrupting exons, one in intron 1 and one in intron 22, each flanked by canonical GT and AG splice sites and each introducing a premature termination codon, and their conservation differs sharply from the known exon, with 20N well conserved in mammals, 22N conserved in only some mammals and 1N not conserved outside primates, lying inside a short interspersed nuclear element.

## how far the zorevunersen evidence reaches

[NEU-358] @zorevunersen2026criticalreview (2026) conf=medium  [ABSTRACT ONLY]
  A narrative review applying explicit criteria to the same evidence concluded that it supports a hypothesis of disease modification without establishing it, noting that at month 36 clinicians and caregivers each rated 94.7 per cent of patients with available assessments as at least minimally improved and that least-squares mean Vineland-3 raw-score changes were 7.56 for the expressive and 6.06 for the receptive subdomain, while dose-group comparisons were post hoc, confidence intervals were not adjusted for multiplicity and late extension outcomes rested on available data with no concurrent control.

## how many genes the platform could address

[NEU-365] @lim2020tango (2020) conf=high
  The addressable set was counted before any drug existed: analysis of RNA sequencing data identified non-productive splicing events in 7,757 human protein-coding genes, of which 1,246 were disease-associated, which is the population of genes this strategy could in principle be aimed at.

## how much of SCN1A output the poison exon diverts

[NEU-342] @lim2020tango (2020) conf=high
  The SCN1A non-productive cassette exon diverts an unusually large share of the gene's output: in neural progenitor cells where decay had been blocked with cycloheximide, the non-productive form was 89 per cent of all SCN1A transcript, the highest of fourteen validated non-productive events whose range ran from 18.6 per cent for MRGBP up to that value.

## how often the drug has to be given again

[NEU-387] @isrctn12811235longwing (2026) conf=high
  The extension record is where the redosing interval is written down: patients rolling over from ADMIRAL into LONGWING (ISRCTN12811235, sponsor study STK-001-DS-502) receive intrathecal STK-001 every four months, with an initial dose on day 1 and further doses at week 16 and week 32, and those tolerating treatment continue every four months, which the record also states as every 16 weeks, with an end-of-study or follow-up visit 24 weeks after the last dose and a duration of participation of up to 15 months or open-ended while tolerated.

## independent corroboration of SCN1A target engagement

[NEU-354] @dawickimckenna2023syngap1 (2023) conf=medium
  A group that shares no author with the programme's other papers reproduced the target engagement as an incidental control: injecting the splice-switching oligonucleotide STK-135 intracerebroventricularly into mouse brain at postnatal day 2 and harvesting five days later improved productive Scn1a splicing and raised Scn1a expression, with no effect on Syngap1 splicing or mRNA.

## limits of the preclinical evidence

[NEU-352] @wagnon2020tangocommentary (2021) conf=high
  The commentator's own list of what the mouse study did not do is the shape of the gap: the animals were treated close to seizure onset at postnatal day 14 but never after seizures had begun, were not followed beyond 90 days of age, and no cognitive or behavioural comorbidity outcome was reported, leaving open when treatment must start, whether a therapeutic window closes, and whether repeated or lifelong dosing would be needed.

## magnitude and duration of a single dose, read secondhand

[NEU-351] @wagnon2020tangocommentary (2021) conf=medium
  The same commentary gives the magnitudes and the duration of a single dose: the most active oligonucleotide raised productive SCN1A transcript more than fifteen-fold in cell culture without changing SCN2A, SCN3A, SCN8A or SCN9A expression; in wild-type mice productive transcript rose dose-dependently to about six-fold five days after one injection; and raised expression was still present at least 30 days after treatment, with up to a twenty-five-fold increase in productive Scn1a transcript and an approximately 30 per cent increase in NaV1.1 protein against untreated adult mouse brain.

## minigene dissection and oligonucleotide rescue of a second SCN1A poison exon

[NEU-375] @tang2025scn1apoisonexons (2025) conf=high
  The intron 22 exon was tested the same way in a reporter: a minigene carrying SCN1A exons 22 and 23 with the whole intervening intron showed significantly increased inclusion of the 22N exon when it carried the patient variant, across five transfections per construct in HEK293T cells, and one of two oligonucleotides tested against the exon, at 1 micromolar, significantly reduced the 22N-containing products relative to the canonical isoform.

## mutation independence of the approach

[NEU-369] @venkatesh2024stk002 (2024) conf=high
  Because the drug acts on the healthy allele, it worked regardless of which mutation the patient carried: STK-002 reduced non-productive splicing and raised OPA1 protein in skin fibroblasts from three unrelated patients with autosomal dominant optic atrophy carrying three different pathogenic OPA1 variants, as well as in wild-type fibroblasts, and improved measures of mitochondrial respiration in the patient cells.

## off-target splicing from a splice-switching oligonucleotide

[NEU-202] @ottesen2021offtarget (2021) conf=high
  One splice-switching oligonucleotide at one high concentration was profiled transcriptome-wide and its specificity did not survive the test: 100 nanomolar Anti-N1, the 20-mer against ISS-N1 that led to nusinersen and a concentration the authors call routine for demonstrating efficacy, substantially stimulated SMN2 exon 7 inclusion in spinal muscular atrophy patient cells over 30 hours but also perturbed the transcriptome and triggered widespread aberrant splicing in genes of transcription, splicing, translation, signalling, the cell cycle, trafficking, cytoskeletal dynamics and innate immunity, verified by PCR on 39 candidate genes, while shorter ISS-N1-targeting oligonucleotides showed substantially fewer off-target effects.
  FIGURE: Two counts from the same experiment: the intended splicing change at SMN2 exon 7, and the number of unintended splicing changes elsewhere, plotted against oligonucleotide length or concentration - so the reader sees specificity as a dose-dependent quantity.

## oligonucleotide blockade of SCN1A exon 20N in cells

[NEU-344] @lim2020tango (2020) conf=high
  Blocking the poison exon raises the gene's productive output in cells: two oligonucleotides taken up by neural progenitor cells without a transfection reagent cut inclusion of the non-productive SCN1A exon dose-dependently and raised productive SCN1A mRNA by an inversely correlated amount, while non-targeting, scrambled and six-mismatch control oligonucleotides at the same concentrations changed neither.

## poison exons as a class in dosage-sensitive genes

[NEU-348] @voskobiynyk2021poisonexon (2021) conf=medium
  The two sodium-channel poison exons are related but not interchangeable: exon 18N of Scn8a shares 37.5 per cent identity with exon 20N of Scn1a in mouse and 57 per cent in human, while the amino acid sequences at their exon boundaries are identical between the two genes and between mouse and human.

## preclinical efficacy of SCN1A poison-exon blockade

[NEU-349] @scn1aaso2020sudepmouse (2020) conf=high  [ABSTRACT ONLY]
  The decisive animal result was a single dose: in the F1:129S-Scn1a+/- by C57BL/6J mouse model of Dravet syndrome, one intracerebroventricular dose of a lead oligonucleotide at postnatal day 2 or at postnatal day 14 lowered the incidence of electrographic seizures and of sudden unexpected death in epilepsy, and productive Scn1a transcript and NaV1.1 protein were confirmed to be raised in the treated brains.

## preclinical survival and seizure figures, read secondhand

[NEU-350] @wagnon2020tangocommentary (2021) conf=medium
  A commentary in Epilepsy Currents, restating that study rather than reporting it, gives the survival figures as about 70 per cent mortality by 30 days of age in untreated Scn1a+/- mice against 97 per cent of mice treated at postnatal day 2 surviving to 90 days, and 85 per cent survival for mice treated at postnatal day 14 against 64 per cent of untreated littermates, with electroencephalography from postnatal day 22 to 46 showing significantly fewer seizures and a longer latency to the first seizure in treated animals.

## seizure frequency was a secondary outcome in the early trials

[NEU-385] @isrctn99651026admiral (2026) conf=high
  The registration settles what the trials were powered to measure: ADMIRAL's primary outcome measures score STK-001 given as multiple doses for safety and tolerability, from screening at day minus 28 until six months after multiple dosing, by adverse events, abnormal vital signs, physical examination findings, 12-lead electrocardiogram and laboratory parameters, together with plasma pharmacokinetics and cerebrospinal fluid exposure of STK-001 by hybridisation ELISA, both of those running from day 1 to six months after multiple dosing, while seizure frequency measured on a paper diary, caregiver and clinician global impression of change and quality of life on the EQ-5D-Y are all secondary outcome measures.

## splice-switching oligonucleotides as isoform-specific tools

[NEU-379] @ezerskiy2022astrocytic4r (2022) conf=high
  An oligonucleotide used in the opposite direction bounds the tau-ratio argument: in mice carrying all six human tau isoforms, a splice-switching oligonucleotide biasing MAPT exon 10 towards inclusion significantly raised 4R tau and lowered 3R tau, significantly increased colocalisation of 4R tau with the astrocyte marker GFAP, and significantly raised brain mRNA of the reactive and neurotoxic genes Vim, Serpina3n, C3 and Serping1, while neither the control oligonucleotide nor the opposite 4R-to-3R oligonucleotide did, and neither oligonucleotide changed those genes in mouse-tau-knockout or wild-type animals.

## the ADMIRAL design and its anticipated enrolment

[NEU-384] @isrctn99651026admiral (2026) conf=high
  The UK half of the first-in-patient programme is registered with a design the American record does not carry: ADMIRAL (ISRCTN99651026, sponsor study STK-001-DS-102) is a phase 1 to 2a open-label study of three dose cohorts at proposed levels of 30, 45 and 70 mg per administration with the option of two further cohorts, giving the assigned dose by intrathecal injection on three separate dosing days, with up to 60 participants anticipated across approximately five to seven UK centres and participation expected to last 40 weeks, or up to 48 if the observation period is extended, comprising about four weeks of screening and observation, about twelve weeks of treatment and about 24 weeks of follow-up.

## the EMPEROR endpoints and dosing as registered

[NEU-360] @nct06872125emperor (2026) conf=high
  EMPEROR's registered outcome measures are seizure change from a daily seizure diary at week 28 and at week 52 and change from baseline in the Vineland Adaptive Behavior Scales third edition, as a multi-component score and as subdomain scores, at week 52; the drug arm receives intrathecal zorevunersen at 70 mg on day 1 and at week 8 and 45 mg at week 24 and week 40, and the sham arm undergoes a procedure that mimics administration without drug.

## the EMPEROR phase 3 design

[NEU-359] @nct06872125emperor (2026) conf=high
  The controlled test is now running: EMPEROR (NCT06872125, EU CTIS 2024-519555-28), a multicentre randomised double-blind sham-controlled parallel-group phase 3 study with an estimated enrolment of 170 patients, assigns patients 1 to 1 to zorevunersen or sham for a first treatment period of about 52 weeks under quadruple masking, after which all eligible patients receive the drug, so no patient stays on sham.

## the EMPEROR phase 3 endpoint as the protocol words it

[NEU-381] @ctis2024519555emperor (2026) conf=high
  The protocol's own wording for the phase 3 endpoint names the measure, the unit and the window: the registered endpoint for EMPEROR in the European register is the frequency of major motor seizures, as a number per 28 days, expressed as percent change from baseline for patients receiving zorevunersen compared with sham, assessed at each visit from week 16 to week 52 across nine consecutive four-week intervals, with net treatment benefit across multiple Vineland-3 outcomes and change from baseline in Vineland-3 subdomain scores, both against sham at week 52, recorded alongside it.

## the TANGO platform and what sets its ceiling

[NEU-364] @lim2020tango (2020) conf=high
  The strategy has a published name and a stated rule: its authors call it TANGO, for targeted-augmentation of nuclear gene output, demonstrated across four genes with 144 oligonucleotides against three classes of non-productive event, and they report that the size of the upregulation an oligonucleotide achieves tracks the abundance of the non-productive event it targets, with a coefficient of determination of 0.9886 and a p-value of 0.0057.

## the baseline population and the cerebrospinal fluid finding

[NEU-363] @balestrini2026dravettherapies (2026) conf=medium
  A 2026 review assembling the programme's numbers describes a population already heavily medicated, with 85 per cent of the 81 patients on three or more antiseizure medications and 54 per cent on four or more and a median baseline convulsive seizure frequency of 17 per 28 days among 77 evaluable patients, and gives drug-related cerebrospinal fluid protein elevation as a treatment-emergent adverse event in 11 of 81 in MONARCH and ADMIRAL and 20 of 74 in SWALLOWTAIL and LONGWING, with transient elevations above 50 mg per decilitre in roughly three-quarters of extension patients when all measurements were counted irrespective of attribution, one discontinuation, and serious adverse events in 22 per cent of patients.

## the first-in-patient trial as registered

[NEU-361] @nct04442295monarch (2025) conf=high
  The registration of the first-in-patient study, MONARCH (NCT04442295, sponsor study STK-001-DS-101), records it as a non-randomised, sequential, open-label phase 1 to 2 study with an actual enrolment of 62 patients that completed on 14 November 2023, and its outcome measures scored STK-001 given as single and multiple doses for safety and tolerability by adverse events, vital signs, examination, electrocardiogram and laboratory parameters from screening at day minus 28, seizure frequency by paper diary over the same window, plasma pharmacokinetics and cerebrospinal fluid exposure from day 1, and a caregiver global impression of change from baseline at day minus 1, each running to six months after dosing.

## the human developmental window for poison-exon therapy

[NEU-373] @tang2025scn1apoisonexons (2025) conf=medium
  The developmental switch was then measured in human brain directly: across human cerebral cortex from fetal life into adulthood, 20N inclusion was high throughout the prenatal period and the early postnatal years before falling markedly in the adult brain, while 1N and 22N stayed below 5 per cent prenatally and showed only a trend downwards after birth.

## the measured ceiling on how much output can be recovered

[NEU-377] @venkatesh2024stk002 (2024) conf=high
  The developers state the ceiling of the class plainly for their second target: the achievable increase may be limited to how abundant the non-productive transcript is in the particular cell type, they could not estimate its true abundance in tissue because decay is running there, and they therefore could not determine how much OPA1 protein increase is achievable in human retina, offering only that mean non-productive abundance was similar in the two species, 3.8 per cent in human retina against 3.57 per cent in monkey retina.

## the platform applied to OPA1 in primate retina

[NEU-368] @venkatesh2024stk002 (2024) conf=high
  The same logic was carried to a second gene and a second organ, and measured in primates: a single bilateral intravitreal injection of STK-002, which blocks a naturally occurring non-productive exon of OPA1, reduced detectable non-productive OPA1 transcript in cynomolgus monkey retina to about 53, 12 and 6 per cent of vehicle at 0.1, 0.3 and 1.0 mg per eye at four weeks, and raised retinal OPA1 protein by 31, 47 and 44 per cent at those doses, with the two higher doses still elevated at eight weeks.

## the platform applied to genes other than SCN1A

[NEU-366] @lim2020tango (2020) conf=high
  Three further genes were taken through the same loop in named cell lines: an oligonucleotide against the non-productive cassette exon 14X of PCCA and one against the non-productive alternative 3-prime splice site of SYNGAP1, both in HEK293 cells, and one against the non-productive retained intron of CD274 in Huh7 cells, each cut the non-productive form dose-dependently and raised the productive mRNA dose-dependently, with the targeted events representing 30, 47 and 20 per cent of each gene's transcript after decay was blocked.

## the zorevunersen clinical programme and its denominators

[NEU-355] @zorevunersen2026nejm (2026) conf=high  [ABSTRACT ONLY]
  The oligonucleotide reached patients in two open-label phase 1 to 2a studies, MONARCH (NCT04442295) and ADMIRAL (ISRCTN99651026), which together enrolled 81 patients aged 2 to 18 years with Dravet syndrome on standard antiseizure medication, in a single-ascending-dose cohort given zorevunersen once at 10 to 70 mg and a multiple-ascending-dose cohort given 20 to 70 mg two or three times over three months; 75 of them had entered the open-label extensions SWALLOWTAIL (NCT04740476) and LONGWING (ISRCTN12811235) as of 30 May 2025, receiving 45 mg or less every four months, with safety and pharmacokinetics as the primary analysis and clinical effects evaluated alongside.

## the zorevunersen seizure result with its arm and window

[NEU-356] @zorevunersen2026nejm (2026) conf=high  [ABSTRACT ONLY]
  The efficacy signal is a within-patient median and not a controlled comparison: among the patients given 70 mg of zorevunersen as one, two or three doses in MONARCH or ADMIRAL and then up to 45 mg in SWALLOWTAIL or LONGWING, convulsive-seizure frequency, as a median change from baseline, ran across one-month intervals during the first 20 months of the extension studies from minus 58.82 per cent to minus 90.91 per cent, and how many patients that 70 mg subgroup contained is not stated in the trial publication's abstract.

## what the EMPEROR sham comparator actually involves

[NEU-383] @jrct2071250035emperor (2026) conf=high
  The sham arm is not an empty gesture: the Japanese registration states that the sham group receives no study drug but undergoes a lumbar puncture with cerebrospinal fluid withdrawal, while the zorevunersen group receives intrathecal drug on day 1 after an eight-week baseline period and then at day 57, day 169 and day 281, at 70 mg for the first two doses and 45 mg for the last two.

## what the open-label extension was set up to measure

[NEU-389] @isrctn12811235longwing (2026) conf=high
  The extension's primary outcome set was amended to put a motor measure in it: as revised on 1 August 2023, LONGWING's primary outcome measures score STK-001 given as multiple doses for safety and tolerability, from screening at day minus 1 until six months after multiple dosing, including the incidence of immunogenicity measured as anti-drug antibodies in serum, together with locomotor skill ability on the total score of the Gillette Functional Assessment Questionnaire, as percent change from baseline, while pharmacokinetics, cerebrospinal fluid exposure, seizure frequency on a paper diary, clinician global impression of change and the EQ-5D-Y are secondary.

## whether there is a window for the drug and whether it is known

[NEU-376] @tang2025scn1apoisonexons (2025) conf=medium
  Those authors read their own human developmental data as widening the therapeutic window, arguing that because 20N remains at high levels in the early postnatal period there may be an extended critical window for oligonucleotide therapy during childhood, and they note that whether the three SCN1A poison exons are co-regulated, and what targeting one does to the others, is unknown.

## which EMPEROR endpoint is primary

[NEU-382] @jrct2071250035emperor (2026) conf=high
  The Japanese registration of the same phase 3 trial separates the endpoints structurally: it lists seizure reduction at week 28, assessed by seizure diary, in its primary outcome section, and seizure reduction at week 52, the multi-component Vineland-3 outcome score as a change from baseline at week 52, and Vineland-3 subdomain scores as a change from baseline at week 52 in its secondary outcome section.

## which cell type the oligonucleotide rescues

[NEU-353] @aso84brain2024excitability (2024) conf=high  [ABSTRACT ONLY]
  A surrogate of the clinical oligonucleotide was used to ask which cells the drug fixes: Scn1a+/- mice given one intracerebroventricular injection of ASO-84, which targets splicing of SCN1A exon 20N, at postnatal day 2 had GABAergic signalling, action potential firing and sodium current density all restored in cortical parvalbumin-positive interneurons measured at postnatal days 21 to 25, while in untreated mutant mice the intrinsic excitability of cortical pyramidal neurons was unchanged and the interneurons showed early hyperexcitability followed by hypoexcitability and depolarisation block.

## who the approach cannot treat

[NEU-362] @nct06872125emperor (2026) conf=high
  The eligibility rules draw the ceiling of the whole approach: EMPEROR requires a documented pathogenic, likely pathogenic or uncertain-significance SCN1A variant and will not randomise a patient whose SCN1A testing was negative, excludes any patient with a documented SCN1A variant associated with gain of function, and excludes patients on a maintenance antiseizure medication acting primarily as a sodium channel blocker, which the record attributes to the drug's mechanism of action.

## why the approach needs a functional second allele

[NEU-367] @lim2020tango (2020) conf=high
  The authors state the rule that makes the approach mutation-independent and also bounds it: it can raise protein from any gene that has two wild-type alleles, or one wild-type and one loss-of-function allele, or at least one hypomorphic allele, because what the oligonucleotide does is redirect a healthy allele's own output rather than supply or repair a gene.

## Calibration and thresholds for splice prediction

[NEU-308] @dawes2023splicevault (2023) conf=high
  The recommended operating points differ sharply in what they catch: with RNA from patient specimens as the standard for 86 splice-site variants, 76 of the 86 were called right at SpliceAI's high-sensitivity cut-off of delta 0.20, which is 88 per cent, and 63 of the 86 at delta 0.50, which is 73 per cent.

[NEU-309] @svobodova2026nf1pseudoexon (2026) conf=medium
  On deep intronic variants the same threshold choice trades most of the sensitivity away: across 501 deep intronic variants in NF1 intron 3 assayed by high-throughput minigene, of which eleven were validated as activating a pseudoexon, raising SpliceAI's delta threshold to the 0.5 recommended for clinical use improved precision to 86 per cent but reduced sensitivity to 50 per cent, against 75 and 66.7 per cent at lower thresholds.

[NEU-310] @smith2023splicebenchmark (2023) conf=high
  For most of the tools there is no single defensible cut-off: when the threshold maximising Youden's J was fitted separately on each dataset in the benchmark, the best cut-off moved substantially between exons, between regions of one exon and between variant classes, so a value derived from one of them performed worse on the rest. The three whose thresholds moved least were SQUIRLS, SpliceAI and Pangolin; the widest movers were HAL and ConSpliceML.

## Ranking variants against quantifying their effect

[NEU-316] @jaganathan2026spliceai2 (2026) conf=medium
  Ranking and quantifying come apart on one variant set: among 4,545 variants that created a GT or AG dinucleotide and were shown by matched GTEx RNA-seq to activate a cryptic splice site, against 573,967 that were not, SpliceAI2 detected the active ones at area under the precision-recall curve 0.77 against 0.66 for SpliceAI, while its estimate of how much the cryptic site was used reached only Spearman 0.63, against 0.47 for Pangolin.
  FIGURE: One figure, one variant set: a precision-recall curve beside a scatter of predicted against measured splice-site usage for the same 4,545 variants, to show that the same model is strong at the first task and middling at the second.

[NEU-317] @zeng2022pangolin (2022) conf=high
  Against a reporter assay of rare variants rather than curated clinical ones, the two leading tools recover little of what the assay calls disruptive: on 27,733 exonic and intronic variants from the Exome Aggregation Consortium assayed by sort-seq, of which 3.8 per cent strongly reduced exon inclusion, Pangolin reached area under the precision-recall curve 0.56 and SpliceAI 0.47, and at 80 per cent precision Pangolin recovered only 29 per cent of the disruptive variants.

[NEU-318] @zeng2022pangolin (2022) conf=high
  The best agreement between a sequence model and measured splicing in this chapter's records comes from a single well-measured exon: for FAS exon 6 in a minigene reporter, Pangolin's predicted percentage spliced in agreed with the measured value at Spearman 0.79 across 189 single substitutions and 0.80 across 3,059 combinations of substitutions.

[NEU-319] @cheng2019mmsplice (2019) conf=high
  On disease alleles measured for splicing efficiency rather than exon inclusion, quantitative agreement was moderate: MMSplice predicted the effect of exonic disease mutations on splicing efficiency, measured for matched wild-type and mutant constructs in the MaPSy assay, at Pearson 0.57 in vitro and 0.37 in vivo across a held-out test set of 797 construct pairs.

[NEU-320] @smith2023splicebenchmark (2023) conf=high
  Taken across tools and datasets the agreement between a predictor's score and the measured change in splicing is middling rather than strong: across eight tools scored against measurements aggregated from four saturation massively parallel splicing assay studies, the median Pearson correlation between a tool's score and the measured splicing effect was 0.53, with a range from minus 0.06 to 0.85 depending on the algorithm and the assay.

## Splice-prediction tools and their measured performance

[NEU-035] @jang2022splicetools (2022) conf=high
  Variant interpretation now leans on tools whose accuracy has been measured against experimentally characterised variants: on a benchmark whose positives are NF1 spliceogenic variants and whose negatives are 64 experimentally validated intronic BRCA variants, SpliceAI reached an ROC area under the curve of 0.972 for de novo or cryptic splice site prediction, ahead of all seven other tools compared except SpliceRover at 0.924, from which the difference was not statistically significant. The authors call that two-gene design a possible source of selection bias and note the positives come from a single gene chosen for its unusually high proportion of splice-affecting pathogenic variants.

[NEU-036] @jaganathan2026spliceai2 (2026) conf=medium
  On the OpenSplice massively parallel reporter benchmark, the successor model SpliceAI2 agreed with experimentally measured variant effects at Spearman 0.59, against 0.55 for SpliceAI.

## Splice-prediction tools: inputs, outputs and training sets

[NEU-301] @zeng2022pangolin (2022) conf=high
  Two tools that are routinely compared answer different questions: Pangolin returns, for each candidate site, both the probability that it is spliced and the fraction of a gene's transcripts that use it, in each of heart, liver, brain and testis, whereas on Pangolin's authors' account SpliceAI reports only whether a dinucleotide is a splice site.

[NEU-302] @zeng2022pangolin (2022) conf=high
  Pangolin's labels are measured rather than annotated: it was fitted to sequence together with RNA splicing measurements made in human, rhesus macaque, mouse and rat, whereas on the same authors' account SpliceAI and MMSplice saw human sequence alone.

[NEU-303] @cheng2019mmsplice (2019) conf=high
  The MMSplice authors state the limitation themselves: their model was not fitted wholly on perturbation data, because the donor and acceptor modules learned from GENCODE's annotated boundaries, a source they call observational, while the exon and intron modules were fitted to a massively parallel reporter assay of random sequence.

[NEU-304] @smith2023splicebenchmark (2023) conf=medium
  An independent benchmarking group characterises the field's leading tools the same way, writing that the predictors built by deep learning on gene models achieved the best performance overall when asked to separate splice-disruptive variants from neutral ones in their benchmark set.

[NEU-305] @jaganathan2026spliceai2 (2026) conf=medium
  The successor model SpliceAI2 was fitted, end to end, on measured usage of splice sites and of splice junctions in 314,745 RNA-seq samples drawn from human and nine further mammals, together with transcript usage from 330 long-read samples, so what it learns to predict is observed usage rather than an annotation.

[NEU-307] @smith2023splicebenchmark (2023) conf=high
  Four of the eight predictors benchmarked require a gene model as input, and the gene models are incomplete: 18.0 per cent of protein-coding genes (3,571 of 19,817) have at least one alternate splice site that is expressed and used at 20 per cent or more of transcripts in at least one GTEx tissue yet is absent from SpliceAI's default annotation.

## Where splice predictors fail

[NEU-306] @dawes2023splicevault (2023) conf=high
  An empirical alternative to sequence models performs comparably: ranking the four commonest unannotated splicing events seen around each splice site in population RNA-seq (300K-RNA Top-4) identified 96 per cent (70 of 73) of the exon-skipping events that 86 variants actually caused in patient material, against 85 per cent (61 of 73) for SpliceAI at a delta score of 0.011, the threshold chosen so that both methods made the same number of predictions.

[NEU-311] @smith2023splicebenchmark (2023) conf=high
  The failure is specifically inside exons: across a benchmark of 1,060 splice-disruptive and 2,852 neutral variants, assembled from four saturation massively parallel splicing assay studies contributing 2,230 variants, a saturation genome-editing screen of BRCA1 at the endogenous locus contributing 1,386, and 296 MLH1 variants curated by hand from clinical databases, the median area under the precision-recall curve was 0.773 for intronic variants and 0.419 for exonic ones, even though the measured proportion of splice-disruptive variants was similar in the two compartments at 28.4 per cent in exons and 25.9 per cent in introns.
  FIGURE: A two-panel figure of the same tools' precision-recall performance on intronic against exonic variants, with the measured splice-disruptive fraction printed on each panel, so the gap cannot be read as a difference in how often each compartment matters.

[NEU-312] @drost2025routinerna (2026) conf=high
  Benchmarked against RNA from patients rather than against curated clinical labels, four current predictors came out close to one another: on 243 variants scorable by all four, SpliceAI reached area under the receiver operating characteristic curve 0.894 and under the precision-recall curve 0.94, Pangolin 0.885 and 0.942, SPiP 0.830 and 0.89, and SQUIRLS 0.806 and 0.888.

[NEU-313] @lord2023cagi6 (2023) conf=high
  In a blinded community challenge, twelve submitted methods plus SpliceAI and CADD were scored on 56 clinically ascertained variants whose splicing effect had been determined by RT-PCR on patient blood RNA, and 14 of the 56 (25 per cent) were predicted correctly by half or fewer of the methods, two of them by a single method, while the best overall accuracy reached was 82 per cent.

[NEU-314] @dawes2023splicevault (2023) conf=high
  Both prediction routes produce far more candidate events than occur: for the same 86 patient-validated variants, positive predictive value for exon skipping was 39 per cent for the empirical Top-4 method and 52 per cent for SpliceAI at a delta score of 0.011, and for cryptic activation 24 and 17 per cent respectively.

[NEU-315] @jaganathan2026spliceai2 (2026) conf=medium
  Tissue-level quantification is the weakest of the numbers reported for the newest model: after fine-tuning on the expression of 147 RNA-binding proteins per tissue, so not from sequence alone, SpliceAI2's predicted usage differences across 14,712,948 comparisons of differentially used junctions between pairs of 48 GTEx tissues correlated with the observed ones at Pearson 0.44, rising to 0.58 for 13,770 junctions between cerebellum and blood.

[NEU-321] @smith2023splicebenchmark (2023) conf=high
  The tools are not independent witnesses: across the benchmark variants the median pairwise Pearson correlation between the eight tools' absolute scores was 0.58 with a range from 0.04 to 0.97, and SpliceAI and Pangolin, which those authors describe as having similar architectures and similar training sets, were almost perfectly correlated with each other at 0.97.

## Retinitis pigmentosa splicing factors and the retina-restricted phenotype

[NEU-233] @splicingfactormousemodels2011rpe (2011) conf=high  [ABSTRACT ONLY]
  Mice carrying the commonest human disease mutations knocked into their own Prpf3 and Prpf8 genes, and mice heterozygous for a Prpf31 knockout, all developed changes in the retinal pigment epithelium - loss of basal infoldings, vacuolisation and deposits between the epithelium and Bruch's membrane by two years in the knock-ins and by one year in the Prpf31 heterozygotes - with decreased rod function reported for the Prpf3-T494M line, from which those authors argue the pigment epithelium may be the primary cell type affected in the splicing-factor forms of retinitis pigmentosa.

[NEU-234] @splicingfactormousemodels2011rpe (2011) conf=high  [ABSTRACT ONLY]
  Those authors open by saying the route from a mutation in a gene used by every cell to a disease confined to the eye had not been worked out, which is the question the retina-restricted phenotype of the splicing-factor genes poses and which they set out to investigate.

[NEU-235] @prpf31mousemodels2009 (2009) conf=high  [ABSTRACT ONLY]
  The negative result that any account of PRPF31 has to accommodate is that mice heterozygous for the human p.A216P knock-in and mice heterozygous for a Prpf31 knockout showed no degenerative phenotype on fundus examination, while both homozygous genotypes were embryonic lethal, which led those authors to conclude that p.A216P is not dominant negative in mouse and that one wild-type allele suffices for a healthy mouse retina.

[NEU-236] @zimmann2025prpf8organoid (2025) conf=medium
  Human induced pluripotent stem cell retinal organoids carrying the PRPF8 p.Tyr2334Asn variant developed photoreceptor defects including a thinner outer segment layer, with only minor changes in messenger RNA levels across retinal cell types and splicing changes concentrated in genes linked to neural and retinal disease, which those authors read as photoreceptor damage arising within the neural retina independently of the retinal pigment epithelium.

[NEU-237] @rose2016msr1penetrance (2016) conf=medium
  The incomplete penetrance of PRPF31 disease tracks a linked common variant rather than the mutation itself: the number of copies of an MSR1 minisatellite next to the promoter varies between three and four in the population, three copies repressed gene transcription in vitro by 50 to 115-fold, and the higher-expressing four-copy allele was not seen in any symptomatic PRPF31 mutation carrier, its frequency instead tracking the proportion of asymptomatic carriers across populations.

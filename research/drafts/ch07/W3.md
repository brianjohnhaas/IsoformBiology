## ISS-N1 and the design of nusinersen

[NEU-183] @smn2006intronicsilencer (2006) conf=high  [ABSTRACT ONLY]
  The element the approved oligonucleotide would later be aimed at was found by dissection rather than by inspection: a sequence immediately downstream of the exon 7 5-prime splice site, inside SMN2 intron 7 and not conserved in evolution, was named intronic splicing silencer N1, its deletion promoted exon 7 inclusion from an SMN2 minigene, and an antisense oligonucleotide annealing to it restored inclusion both from the minigene and from endogenous SMN2 while raising SMN protein in patient-derived cells that carry only SMN2.
  FIGURE: SMN2 exon 7 with its flanking intron, marking the enhancer, the silencer and ISS-N1, with each element's measured effect on inclusion when removed - so the reader sees why an intronic element downstream of the exon became the drug target.

## SMN1/SMN2 and spinal muscular atrophy

[NEU-015] @singh2020risdiplam (2020) conf=high
  SMN2 is a nearly identical copy of SMN1 that fails to compensate for its loss because it predominantly skips exon 7, and the change held responsible is a single C-to-T substitution at the sixth nucleotide of that exon, proposed to weaken its 3' splice site.
  FIGURE: A side-by-side of SMN1 and SMN2 exon 7 marking the single divergent nucleotide, with the downstream intronic silencer that the approved oligonucleotide targets marked on the same diagram.

[NEU-016] @singh2020risdiplam (2020) conf=high
  Both approved splicing-directed SMA drugs act on the same exon: nusinersen is an antisense oligonucleotide targeting the intronic splicing silencer ISS-N1 in SMN2 and is described as the first FDA-approved drug for SMA, while risdiplam, an orally deliverable small molecule, became the third approved SMA therapy.

[NEU-017] @finkel2017endear (2017) conf=high  [ABSTRACT ONLY]
  In the phase 3 sham-controlled trial of nusinersen in infants with spinal muscular atrophy, the final analysis recorded a motor-milestone response in 37 of 73 treated infants (51 per cent) and in none of the 37 controls.

[NEU-018] @belancic2025smatrials (2025) conf=medium
  In FIREFISH Part 1, 41 per cent of the infants with type 1 SMA given the therapeutic dose of risdiplam (7 of 17) could sit independently for at least five seconds at twelve months.

[NEU-019] @belancic2025smatrials (2025) conf=medium
  Risdiplam is described as a once-daily oral SMN2 pre-mRNA splicing modifier that raises full-length SMN protein by promoting exon 7 inclusion, and it received EMA approval in March 2021 on the basis of the FIREFISH and SUNFISH trials.

## SMN2 copy number and severity

[NEU-184] @fdanusinersenreview2016 (2016) conf=high
  In untreated spinal muscular atrophy the regulator's review ties SMN2 copy number to outcome in steps: two copies correspond to uniform failure to sit unassisted and rare survival beyond 24 months without life-sustaining intervention, three copies to general inability to walk without assistance with roughly 70 per cent alive at 25 years, and four or more copies to possibly normal life expectancy with a varied course.

[NEU-185] @smn22002copynumber (2002) conf=high  [ABSTRACT ONLY]
  Copy number sets the odds and not the outcome: among 27 patients homozygous for SMN1 deletion, mean SMN2 copy number was 3.1 plus or minus 0.3 in the 16 with type II or III disease against 2.2 plus or minus 0.6 in the 11 with type I (P less than 0.01), yet three of those 11 type I patients carried three copies, and transcripts from one such patient were mostly the exon-7-skipped form with only a trace of full-length SMN2 mRNA.

## SMN2 exon 7: two competing mechanisms

[NEU-181] @smn22002sf2asfese (2002) conf=high  [ABSTRACT ONLY]
  One explanation for why SMN2 includes exon 7 poorly is subtractive: the single C-to-T difference falls inside a heptamer of an exonic splicing enhancer that SF2/ASF binds directly in SMN1, so the substitution is held to remove an activator rather than to add a repressor, a conclusion reached from enhancer motif prediction, mutational analysis and splicing assays in cells and in vitro.
  FIGURE: Three panels of the same exon 7 sequence: the enhancer reading, the silencer reading, and what each predicts would happen if the trans-acting factor were removed - so the reader sees that the two models differ in a testable way rather than in emphasis.

[NEU-182] @smn22003negativeelement (2003) conf=high  [ABSTRACT ONLY]
  A second explanation contradicts the first: the same C-to-T transition is reported to create an exonic splicing silencer in SMN2 rather than to destroy an enhancer in SMN1, with hnRNP A1 binding SMN2 but not SMN1 exon 7 RNA, and lowering hnRNP A protein with small interfering RNAs in living cells restored efficient SMN2 exon 7 splicing.

## branaplam, SMN2 and HTT

[NEU-199] @gubserkeller2022branaplam (2022) conf=high
  The same chemistry was turned on a second gene: branaplam, developed as an SMN2 splicing modifier for spinal muscular atrophy, promotes inclusion of a pseudoexon in HTT transcripts, which lowered mutant huntingtin protein in patient cells, in a mouse model of Huntington disease and in blood from type 1 SMA patients dosed orally for SMA, and reanalysis of the authors' own RNA sequencing showed that branaplam induces splicing changes beyond the exons it was already known to include, with the exons it favours enriched for a non-canonical nGA motif at the 3-prime end of the exon.

[NEU-200] @borowsky2026vibranthd (2026) conf=high
  The Huntington programme stopped on toxicity rather than on futility: in the randomised phase 2b trial VIBRANT-HD, peripheral neuropathy appeared, as a sign or a symptom or both, in 18 of the 21 participants (85.7 per cent) of the first cohort, who were given branaplam 56 milligrams weekly, and that signal together with dose modelling ended the trial early, so the primary outcome, a fall in cerebrospinal fluid mutant huntingtin against placebo, could only be summarised descriptively.

[NEU-201] @krach2025neuropathy (2025) conf=high
  Work in human induced pluripotent stem cell-derived motor neurons puts the neuropathy on the molecule rather than on the schedule: branaplam activated p53, produced nucleolar stress, raised expression of the p53 target BBC3 and disrupted neurite integrity with elevated neurofilament light chain, which those authors read as a small therapeutic window between lowering huntingtin and an off-target neuropathy.

## gene replacement as the contrast to splice modulation

[NEU-196] @sma2017singledose (2017) conf=high  [ABSTRACT ONLY]
  One SMA therapy does not act on splicing at all: it delivers SMN complementary DNA in an adeno-associated virus serotype 9 vector, and in the trial reported here, registered as NCT02122952, 15 infants with type 1 disease received one intravenous dose, 3 at 6.7 times ten to the thirteenth and 12 at 2.0 times ten to the fourteenth vector genomes per kilogram, with safety as the primary outcome; none of the 15 had died or come to need permanent ventilation by 20 months of age, where a historical cohort put survival at 8 per cent.

[NEU-197] @strauss2022spr1nt (2022) conf=high
  Presymptomatic gene replacement was tested in SPR1NT, a single-arm phase 3 trial in which all 14 children with two SMN2 copies dosed at six weeks of life or earlier sat independently for at least 30 seconds at some visit by 18 months, scored as Bayley-III item 26 (P less than 0.001), 11 of them within the normal developmental window, against a matched natural-history cohort of 23; the mechanism is gene replacement rather than splice modulation.

## how a small molecule shifts a splice choice

[NEU-198] @malard2024bulgerepair (2024) conf=high
  The two classes of splicing drug act at different points on the same decision: antisense oligonucleotides work indirectly by occupying cis-regulatory elements or breaking inhibitory structures, while small molecules act directly at the interface between U1 small nuclear ribonucleoprotein and the 5-prime splice site, and solution structures of risdiplam, branaplam and two related compounds place all of them on an RNA helix epitope containing an unpaired adenine in a G-2 A-1 G+1 U+2 motif. Those authors report that the four contact the epitope similarly to the prototype compound for which bulge repair was established, which they say suggests the mechanism generalises.
  FIGURE: A weak 5-prime splice site with an unpaired adenine bulging out of the duplex it forms with U1 small nuclear RNA, and the same site with a small molecule wedged in, so the reader sees that the drug is not editing sequence but holding a shape.

## presymptomatic treatment and newborn screening

[NEU-193] @nurture2023fiveyear (2023) conf=high  [ABSTRACT ONLY]
  Presymptomatic nusinersen was tested without a control arm: NURTURE gave nusinersen to 15 children with two SMN2 copies and 10 with three before symptoms appeared, and at a median age of 4.9 years at last visit none had discontinued, all were alive, all 15 of those with two copies had achieved sitting without support, 14 of 15 walking with assistance and 13 of 15 walking alone, against a primary endpoint of time to death or respiratory intervention.

[NEU-194] @rainbowfish2025presymptomatic (2025) conf=high  [ABSTRACT ONLY]
  RAINBOWFISH treated 26 infants aged from birth to 42 days who had a genetic diagnosis of spinal muscular atrophy but no strongly suggestive clinical signs, and its primary outcome, sitting without support for at least five seconds at month 12 restricted to infants who had two SMN2 copies together with an ulnar compound muscle action potential amplitude at baseline of 1.5 millivolts or more, was reached by 4 of those 5 infants (80 per cent, 95 per cent confidence interval 28 to 100).

[NEU-195] @cooper2024presymptomatic (2024) conf=high
  A systematic review of presymptomatic treatment up to December 2023 found three single-arm interventional trials, six observational studies comparing presymptomatic or screened with symptomatic cohorts and twelve follow-up studies of newborn-screened cohorts, and reports that the comparative observational studies supported better outcomes from presymptomatic treatment and from early treatment after screening than from treatment begun once symptoms were present.

## trial: CHERISH (nusinersen, later-onset SMA)

[NEU-188] @cherish2018nusinersen (2018) conf=high  [ABSTRACT ONLY]
  CHERISH randomised 126 children whose symptoms began after six months of age in a 2:1 ratio to intrathecal nusinersen 12 mg or a sham procedure on days 1, 29, 85 and 274, and its primary endpoint, least-squares mean change from baseline in the Hammersmith Functional Motor Scale-Expanded at 15 months, favoured nusinersen by 5.9 points (95 per cent confidence interval 3.7 to 8.1, P less than 0.001) in the prespecified interim analysis that stopped the trial early.

## trial: FIREFISH Part 1 (risdiplam, type 1 SMA)

  Risdiplam's pharmacodynamic effect was read in blood rather than in motor neurons: in FIREFISH Part 1, median blood SMN protein at twelve months was 3.0 times baseline in the four infants whose final dose at month 12 was 0.08 mg per kilogram per day and 1.9 times baseline in the 17 whose final dose at month 12 was 0.2 mg per kilogram per day, rising from medians of 1.31 to 3.05 and from 2.54 to 5.66 nanograms per millilitre respectively.

## trial: SUNFISH Part 2 (risdiplam, type 2 and 3 SMA)

[NEU-191] @fdarisdiplamreview2020 (2020) conf=high
  SUNFISH Part 2 randomised 180 non-ambulant patients aged 2 to 25 years with type 2 or type 3 spinal muscular atrophy in a 2:1 ratio to once-daily oral risdiplam (120 patients) or placebo (60), stratified by age band, with the primary endpoint defined as change from baseline in the 32-item Motor Function Measure total score at month 12.

## DMD exon skipping: how dystrophin was measured

[NEU-205] @fdaeteplirsenreview2016 (2016) conf=high
  Two assays of the same muscle gave answers the reviewer judged not to agree: in eteplirsen Study 201/202 the sponsor's immunohistochemistry was reported as rising from 1.1 plus or minus 1.3 per cent dystrophin-positive fibres at baseline to 17.4 plus or minus 10.0 per cent at week 180, while western blot put week 180 dystrophin at a mean of only 0.93 per cent of normal, and the reviewer called the lack of concordance between the two striking; repeat biopsies were taken in 11 of 12 patients, only 3 of those 11 had evaluable stored baseline samples, six further baselines came from patients outside the study, and all baselines were taken from a different muscle group.
  FIGURE: The same cohort scored two ways on one axis - per cent dystrophin-positive fibres against per cent of normal dystrophin by western blot - with a line connecting each patient's two values, so the reader sees that the disagreement is structural and not noise.

[NEU-206] @fdaeteplirsenreview2016 (2016) conf=high
  The reason the two assays cannot be compared is in the counting rule: any fibre showing staining perceptibly above background counts as positive, so the proportion of positive fibres says nothing about how much dystrophin a patient carries against a healthy individual, as a proportion or as an amount, and a reading of 50 per cent positive fibres means only that half the fibres stained above background.

[NEU-208] @fdaviltolarsenreview2020 (2020) conf=high
  Viltolarsen's dystrophin was measured by two assays that disagree by roughly twofold on the same samples: after 24 weekly doses in 8 patients per dose group, the median increase in truncated dystrophin was 4.6 per cent of normal at 40 milligrams per kilogram per week and 3.8 per cent at 80 by the primary western blot endpoint, normalised to myosin heavy chain, but 1.7 and 1.9 per cent respectively by mass spectrometry as a secondary endpoint, and the reviewer supported approval of the higher dose on the marginally higher mass spectrometry value. A third endpoint in the same review, immunofluorescence counting of dystrophin-positive myofibres, gave mean increases of 12.8 and 33.0 per cent at week 25.

## DMD exon skipping: the dystrophin numbers

[NEU-204] @fdagolodirsenreview2019 (2019) conf=high
  The eteplirsen approval figure, as the regulator states it: 13 patients had baseline muscle biopsies, were given 30 milligrams per kilogram weekly for 48 weeks and were reassessed by western blot, and in the 12 with evaluable results dystrophin rose from 0.16 plus or minus 0.12 per cent of normal to 0.44 plus or minus 0.43 per cent (p less than 0.05), a median increase of 0.1 per cent of normal, which the reviewer glosses as one part in a thousand.

[NEU-207] @fdagolodirsenreview2019 (2019) conf=high
  Golodirsen serves patients amenable to exon 53 skipping, about 8 per cent of the Duchenne population, and in Study 4053-101 dystrophin in the 25 treated patients rose from a baseline of 0.095 per cent of normal to 1.019 per cent at Part 2 week 48, a mean increase of 0.924 per cent with a median change of 0.88 per cent, with 24 of the 25 baseline biopsies below the assay's lower limit of quantification of 0.25 per cent, 18 of 25 above it at week 48, individual week 48 values from 0.09 to 4.30 per cent and minimal correlation between dystrophin level and how long the patient had been treated.

[NEU-209] @fdacasimersenreview2021 (2021) conf=high
  Casimersen's approval figure is a between-arm difference rather than a within-patient change: at an interim analysis of study 4045-301 after 48 weeks of double-blind treatment, 27 of the first 43 patients to reach the timepoint were on casimersen and 16 on placebo, and western blot of muscle biopsy showed a mean dystrophin difference of 0.59 per cent of normal and a 1.6 per cent increase in exon 45 skipping, both statistically significant, with the trial's 6-minute walk test data not yet available and exon-45-amenable mutations put at about 8 per cent of Duchenne patients.

## DMD exon skipping: who each drug is for

[NEU-203] @fdaeteplirsenreview2016 (2016) conf=high
  Each Duchenne oligonucleotide serves only the slice of the disease defined by the skip it performs: the Commissioner's decision on the eteplirsen dispute states that mutations amenable to exon 51 skipping affect about 13 per cent of the population with Duchenne muscular dystrophy.

## DMD exon-skipping therapy

[NEU-020] @lim2017eteplirsen (2017) conf=high
  The therapeutic logic of exon skipping in Duchenne dystrophy came from reading a natural experiment: DMD deletions that leave the reading frame intact generate variants able to produce a functional if truncated dystrophin and the milder Becker phenotype, which is read as showing that the nature of the deletion determines severity.
  FIGURE: A frame diagram: an out-of-frame deletion, the same deletion with one extra exon skipped, and the resulting in-frame product - making visible why removing more sequence can help.

[NEU-021] @moriyama2026dmdaso (2026) conf=high
  Four exon-skipping antisense drugs for Duchenne dystrophy hold FDA approval, but these were accelerated approvals resting mainly on a biomarker - modest increases in dystrophin - and that basis is described as having sparked significant debate.

## the dystrophin surrogate: the confirmatory results

[NEU-214] @essence2026topline (2026) conf=medium  [ABSTRACT ONLY]
  The confirmatory trial for two of the four did not meet its endpoint: ESSENCE enrolled 225 ambulatory boys amenable to exon 53 or exon 45 skipping, randomised 2:1 to golodirsen or casimersen at 30 milligrams per kilogram or to placebo, and in the 138 analysed in the combined treatment group against 74 on placebo the primary endpoint, change from baseline in 4-step ascend velocity at week 96, differed by 0.06 steps per second (95 per cent confidence interval minus 0.05 to 0.16, P equal to 0.309), while dystrophin by western blot at week 96 differed by 0.69 percentage points of normal unadjusted (0.28 to 1.10, P less than 0.001) and by 1.25 percentage points adjusted for muscle content (0.20 to 2.31, P equal to 0.020).
  FIGURE: One panel per drug with two axes: the dystrophin change the approval rested on, and the functional endpoint measured later, so the reader sees the surrogate move and the function not move in the same trial.

[NEU-215] @fdagolodirsenreview2019 (2019) conf=high
  Eteplirsen's required confirmatory trial had not been started three years after the drug reached the market: the approval letter obliged the sponsor to conduct a two-year randomised double-blind trial comparing the approved 30 milligrams per kilogram weekly against a dose seven times higher, with the North Star Ambulatory Assessment as primary endpoint, and the reviewing office recorded that almost three years after the 2016 approval the company had not initiated it, so that nothing more was known about the drug's clinical efficacy than at approval.

## the dystrophin surrogate: the disagreement

[NEU-210] @fdaeteplirsenreview2016 (2016) conf=high
  One side of the dystrophin argument, in the regulator's own words: the Director of the Office of Drug Evaluation I set the bar at functional protein of roughly 10 per cent of normal before any increase could be called reasonably likely to predict clinical benefit, reasoning from Becker dystrophy as a model, and supplied his own plots and arithmetic, on his reading of which the eteplirsen trials show no association between how much dystrophin a boy made and how well he moved.

[NEU-211] @fdaeteplirsenreview2016 (2016) conf=high
  The other side, from the same document: the Center Director held that no particular dystrophin threshold could be named on any rational basis, citing assay validation, the difficulty of knowing whether the protein made is functional and the difficulty of relating an amount of protein to function over time, and offered post-hoc calculations from the eteplirsen trials that she regarded as supportive but not definitive evidence that higher dystrophin went with greater function.

[NEU-212] @fdagolodirsenreview2019 (2019) conf=high
  The second of the four approvals was first refused: the Office Director's decisional memorandum of 19 August 2019 on golodirsen records a complete response action, and states that the review division's memorandum supported accelerated approval solely on the precedent of the 2016 eteplirsen approval, never asserting that the quantity of dystrophin golodirsen produces is reasonably likely to predict clinical benefit and never addressing what quantity would be.

[NEU-213] @fdaviltolarsenreview2020 (2020) conf=high
  The third approval says the same of itself: the viltolarsen review recommends accelerated approval on the precedent of the eteplirsen and golodirsen approvals, having stated that the applicant did not provide adequate empirical evidence that the dystrophin levels viltolarsen produces, median 1.9 to 3.8 per cent of normal by mass spectrometry and western blot, are reasonably likely to confer clinical benefit.

## the reading-frame rule and its exceptions

[NEU-216] @dmd2006leidendatabase (2006) conf=high  [ABSTRACT ONLY]
  The reading-frame rule is a strong generalisation rather than a law: of more than 4,700 DMD mutations then recorded in the Leiden mutation database, 91 per cent agreed with the rule that frameshifting mutations give the severe Duchenne phenotype and in-frame mutations the milder Becker phenotype, which leaves roughly one in eleven that did not.
  FIGURE: Two bars for the same rule measured on different denominators - all database entries against a Becker-ascertained biopsy series - so the reader sees the exception rate as a property of the cohort as much as of the gene.

[NEU-217] @bmd2008framerule (2008) conf=high  [ABSTRACT ONLY]
  Ascertainment moves that figure sharply: in a series of 75 patients whose muscle biopsies showed abnormal dystrophin consistent with Becker dystrophy, a disease-causing mutation was found in 66 (88 per cent), and 17 of the 56 deletions or duplications (30 per cent) were out of frame, which the authors attribute partly to a high incidence of deletions at the 5-prime end of the gene, a known hotspot for exceptions, and partly to complex splicing in which a subset of transcripts lost more sequence than the genomic deletion.

## One patient, one drug {#sec-nof1}

### Milasen and the programmes beside it

The endpoint of isoform-level diagnosis is a drug made for one genome. Cell
lines from a single patient supplied the proof-of-concept work behind milasen, a
splice-modulating oligonucleotide taken into an n-of-1 study within one year of
first contact with that patient [@kim2019milasen]. The design route ran from
prediction to patient cells to rodent toxicology. Candidate 2'-methoxyethyl and
2'-O-methyl oligonucleotides were chosen by in silico prediction, screened in
the patient's own fibroblasts for the proportion of healthy CLN7 transcript, and
given to rats at 2.5, 10 and 42 times the intended dose for 70 days, where the
high-dose animals showed dorsal root ganglion toxicity and gait disturbance that
then shaped clinical monitoring. On the same 2024 review's account, the drug was
aimed at a cryptic splice site created by a novel insertion in intron 6 of the
second MFSD8 allele [@wiltonclark2024nof1aso].

What the primary report asserts is narrower than the case's reputation. It
reaches this book as an abstract, recording no serious adverse events and a fall
in seizures that it calls objective, ascertained both from electroencephalography
and from parental report, in a single-patient design with no control arm and no
comparison but the patient's own pre-treatment state [@kim2019milasen]. That
abstract gives no magnitude, no timepoint and no treatment duration. The figure
usually quoted, seizure frequency and duration falling to half their
pre-treatment values, comes from the review, which also reports that the drug did
not impede progressive brain volume loss and that the patient died three years
after treatment began [@wiltonclark2024nof1aso].

Atipeksen, the splice-switching n-of-1 drug that followed, was given
intrathecally under an expanded access protocol to a child with
ataxia-telangiectasia from the age of two, with exploratory dose escalation and
then 42 milligrams every three months. It had been tolerated for three years
without serious adverse events at the time of report [@kim2023asoframework]. The
correction behind it was partial in cells and came with a second effect. In
fibroblasts carrying the ATM c.7865C>T allele the two lead oligonucleotides
raised functional ATM transcript from 0 to 5 per cent untreated to 29 per cent
and 18 per cent, while both also increased skipping of exon 53. Serious harms
have appeared in the same clinical territory, whether or not the drug caused
them. One investigational protocol for a knockdown oligonucleotide in KCNT1
epileptic encephalopathy was joined by two patients ten months apart. The first
was found to have severe communicating hydrocephalus after symptoms that began
eight weeks past her ninth intrathecal dose, her family then withdrew in favour
of palliative care, and she died three months after the final dose. The second
developed status dystonicus and needed a ventriculoperitoneal shunt, after which
dosing was paused and resumed only two years later at lower doses
[@nakayama2026kcnt1aso]. These three reports are not three independent bodies of
experience. Their bylines share many name strings, the senior author among them,
and the milasen report is here an abstract, so the overlap can be read at the
level of names rather than of confirmed authorship.

Single-patient studies do publish missed endpoints. In two parallel n-of-1
studies of individualised allele-selective oligonucleotides in SCN2A
developmental and epileptic encephalopathy, each patient was compared against
their own pre-treatment period. The first patient's estimated 26 per cent
reduction in seizure count, over 744 days on treatment against 90 days before
it, did not reach significance (beta minus 0.298, 95 per cent confidence interval
minus 0.846 to 0.249, P equals 0.286). The second patient's estimated 90 per cent
reduction, over 480 days against 42 days before it, did
[@kimmcmanus2026scn2aaso]. Both baselines are short, and much shorter than the
treated periods they anchor. The mechanism in that pair is allele knockdown
rather than splice modulation, so it bears on how such programmes report
outcomes rather than on splice correction. The distinction is live: one
foundation says that several of its oligonucleotides in development act by
altering RNA splicing, but that it has not yet accumulated enough clinical
exposure to judge whether they help, and the three patients it presents in detail
received allele-selective or RNase H drugs [@crooke2024nanorareaso].

The route from request to dose is slow, and its denominator is applications
rather than treated patients. Of 173 applications to that foundation for an
experimental individualised oligonucleotide by 2 January 2023, 78 were accepted
for potential treatment and 57 were declined, most often because no antisense
approach existed for the mutation. Across the first eight investigational new
drug applications the median time from acceptance to first dose was 23 months
against the foundation's own 18-month target [@crooke2024nanorareaso], which is a
small base for a median. Both counts are the foundation's own, published with a
declared commercial relationship with a pharmaceutical company. The regulatory
frame differs by continent. FDA draft guidance asks a sponsor-investigator to
request a pre-investigational-new-drug meeting once both a participant and one or
more candidate individualised oligonucleotides, backed by convincing
proof-of-concept data, are in hand [@fda2021individualizedaso]. For Europe, one
group argues that marketing authorisation under exceptional circumstances is not
feasible for a one-patient oligonucleotide, leaving named-patient supply of an
unlicensed medicine, with most oversight resting on national law
[@synofzik2022nof1europe].

### Three patients in 532

Of 532 patients given a diagnosis by clinical genome-wide sequencing at one
paediatric hospital over six years, 25 unique variants in 26 patients (4.9 per
cent) were eligible or likely eligible for oligonucleotide treatment, by
messenger RNA knockdown in 18, canonical exon skipping in 4 and splice correction
in 3 [@cheerie2025asoscreening]. Three is the figure this book needs when it
calls splice-switching tractable for few patients. The consensus scheme applied
there reserves its top tier for variants against which an oligonucleotide has
already been developed and shown effective, clinically or preclinically
[@cheerie2025variantguidelines], so the count tracks demonstrated precedent as
much as biology, and the screen is not an independent test of criteria its own
group wrote. A larger fraction exists for one recessive disease under a different
taxonomy: whole-genome sequencing of 235 individuals with ataxia-telangiectasia
from 209 families placed 15 per cent in the probably or possibly amenable classes
the authors defined [@kim2023asoframework]. The two numbers answer different
questions, and the smaller one is the one a clinic would meet.

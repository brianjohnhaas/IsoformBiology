## Neurexin splice sites and synapse function

[NEU-128] @trotter2023neurexin3 (2023) conf=high
  Two neurexin splice sites act as a hierarchical code rather than as independent switches: rescuing neurexin-3 conditional knockout neurons showed that a variant lacking the splice site 4 insert restores inhibitory transmission if splice site 2 either lacks an insert or carries only the short one, whereas a variant carrying the splice site 4 insert restores it only when splice site 2 has no insert at all, so one of the two sites must be insert-free.

[NEU-129] @trotter2023neurexin3 (2023) conf=high
  The splice code works by deciding whether a binding partner is engaged, and engaging it is the whole requirement: in olfactory bulb and prefrontal cortex, irrespective of sex, alternative splicing at neurexin-3 splice sites 2 and 4 left inhibitory synapses unchanged in number while altering their release probability; inhibitory synapse function was sustained by variants that let neurexin-3 bind dystroglycan and not by variants that do not bind it, and a minimal neurexin-3 protein that binds dystroglycan was enough on its own.

## FUS, SOD1, C9orf72 and FTLD-TAU as comparison groups

[NEU-174] @humphrey2017crypticquant (2017) conf=high
  The cryptic-exon mechanism does not generalise to the other ALS RNA-binding protein: the pipeline that classified scores of human cryptic exons after TDP-43 depletion classified only one human and three mouse cryptic exons after FUS depletion, and no cassette-like cryptic exon in either species, although the FUS datasets matched the TDP-43 datasets in cell type, depletion method, sequencing depth and laboratory of origin.

[NEU-175] @humphrey2020fusintrons (2020) conf=high

## Isoform-specific functional difference between 3R and 4R tau

[NEU-105] @falcon2018pickfold (2018) conf=high
  The isoform restriction in Pick disease is a property of the seed and not only of what the cell expresses: the sarkosyl-insoluble fraction from the Pick disease brain used for cryo-electron microscopy seeded aggregation of recombinant full-length 0N3R tau but not of 0N4R tau, across three independent experiments with separate protein preparations, and the fold cannot accommodate the bulkier K294 and V300 of the second repeat in place of T263 and Q269 of the first.

[NEU-107] @zhang2020cbdfold (2020) conf=high
  The seeding asymmetry runs in both directions: full-length 4R human tau, expressed in SH-SY5Y cells, let the sarkosyl-insoluble fraction from the three corticobasal degeneration brains seed aggregation of soluble tau, while cells expressing 3R did not, the reverse of the Pick disease result, and the structural reason the authors suggest is that where the last residue of the second repeat sits cannot accommodate the last residue of the first repeat.

[NEU-115] @adams2010tauassembly (2010) conf=high
  Mixing the two tau isoforms suppresses aggregation that either alone does not: heparin-induced polymerisation in vitro was lower for combinations of 3R and 4R tau than for 4R preparations alone, adding even small amounts of 3R to a 4R assembly reaction significantly decreased 4R assembly, and the effect held whether or not the reaction was reducing and whether or not the N-terminal inserts encoded by exons 2 and 3 were present.

[NEU-116] @microtubuledynamics2004taulivingcells (2004) conf=high  [ABSTRACT ONLY]
  The two tau isoforms stabilise microtubules to measurably different degrees inside cells: physiological amounts microinjected into cultured MCF7 cells expressing EGFP-tubulin suppressed microtubule dynamics for both isoforms, yet 3R tau's potency in stabilising most dynamic parameters was about threefold below that of 4R tau, and it barely suppressed shortening events at all.

[NEU-117] @dynamicinstability2005tausubpopulations (2005) conf=high  [ABSTRACT ONLY]
  The difference between 3R and 4R tau on microtubules is qualitative and concentration-dependent rather than a simple ranking: by video microscopy of individual microtubules in vitro, at tau-to-tubulin ratios of 1:55 and 1:45 every 3R isoform reduced growth rates relative to a no-tau control while every 4R isoform raised them, yet at 1:20 both raised them, with 4R shifting microtubules into a faster-growing subpopulation at lower ratios than 3R.

[NEU-122] @hung2023vcp4rtau (2024) conf=high
  A splice-switching antisense oligonucleotide that shifts 3R tau to 4R, applied at 3.5 micromolar to the medium of cortical neurons derived from three control induced-pluripotent-stem-cell lines for ten days, significantly raised 4R tau protein and significantly raised tau phosphorylation at Ser404 and at Ser202/Thr205, reproducing what VCP-mutant neurons show. Because the oligonucleotide lowers 3R as it raises 4R, what was tested is a shift in the ratio rather than an increase in one isoform on its own.

[NEU-123] @hung2023vcp4rtau (2024) conf=high
  The same 3R-to-4R shift drove the rest of the cascade: cortical neurons from three control lines treated with the splice-switching oligonucleotide showed significantly larger early endosomes and lysosomes and more of them above one square micrometre, more Galectin 1 puncta marking lysosomal membrane rupture, raised phosphorylated eIF2alpha and a higher proportion of cleaved-caspase-3-positive cells.

## MAPT exon 10 and the 3R/4R balance

[NEU-001] @liu2008tauexon10 (2008) conf=high
  Adult human brain holds 3R- and 4R-tau at roughly a one-to-one ratio, so the inclusion or skipping of a single MAPT exon - exon 10, which encodes the second microtubule-binding repeat - is what sets the balance between the two halves of the tau isoform pool.
  FIGURE: One axis showing 3R:4R ratio, with a vertical band marking the normal adult range near 1, and markers for the direction each disease or mutation class pushes the ratio - so the reader sees that both directions are pathogenic.

[NEU-002] @liu2008tauexon10 (2008) conf=high
  Eleven FTDP-17 mutations fall inside a single self-complementary stem loop spanning the exon 10 / intron 10 boundary of MAPT, and each disrupts the complementarity and destabilises the structure; the same position is already destabilised in rodents, which is offered as the reason adult mice and rats are predominantly 4R.

[NEU-003] @liu2008tauexon10 (2008) conf=high
  The FTDP-17 mutations N279K and delta-280K sit in the same polypurine enhancer of MAPT exon 10 and add or delete an AAG copy respectively, raising or lowering SF2/ASF binding, so that in cultured cells two neighbouring changes push exon 10 inclusion in opposite directions. The review restricts the SR-protein work it is summarising to cultured cells and reports that some of those observations contradict each other, which it attributes to minigene size, cell type and endogenous splicing-factor levels.

[NEU-004] @liu2008tauexon10 (2008) conf=high
  Because some FTDP-17 mutations are silent or intronic and disrupt only MAPT exon 10 splicing without altering tau's primary sequence, an altered 3R:4R ratio is by itself sufficient to cause neurodegeneration and dementia; most such mutations push toward 4R, a minority toward 3R.

## Tau exon 10 cis-elements beyond the stem loop

[NEU-103] @liu2008tauexon10 (2008) conf=medium
  Tau exon 10 is unusual in having two weak splice sites rather than one and in being flanked by a 13.6 kb intron 9 and a 3.8 kb intron 10, and seven short cis-elements have been identified and been characterised in and around it: an SC35-like enhancer, a polypurine enhancer and an A/C-rich enhancer at the exon's 5' end, a silencer after them, a further enhancer at the 3' end, and in intron 10 a bipartite intronic silencer at E10+11 to E10+18 with an intronic splicing modulator at E10+19 to E10+26; fourteen tauopathy mutations fall inside six of those seven elements.

## Therapeutic targeting of tau exon 10 splicing

[NEU-118] @silva2026maptsmc (2026) conf=high
  Small molecules that push MAPT exon 10 out can be made to work in patient neurons: in induced-pluripotent-stem-cell-derived cortical neurons from a frontotemporal dementia patient carrying the tau-P301L mutation, 96 hours of PTC-700 or PTC-775 cut total tau, pTau-S396 and pTau-S202/T205 by 40 to 60 per cent at the highest concentration, three weeks of either reached 90 to 100 per cent, and all three compounds tested removed nearly all oligomeric pTau-S396 at 1 micromolar, across three biological replicates.

[NEU-119] @silva2026maptsmc (2026) conf=high
  The same chemistry corrects the ratio in an animal: in mice whose Mapt locus was replaced by the human MAPT gene carrying the tau-N279K splicing mutation on a mouse tau-null background, homozygotes given PTC-003 by daily oral gavage for 21 days at 1 and 2.5 mg per kg, with a cytochrome P450 inhibitor to raise exposure, showed a dose-dependent rise in the ratio of 3R tau to total tau in brain and a dose-dependent fall in brain pTau-T231, each reaching significance at 2.5 mg per kg.
  FIGURE: Two arrows on one 3R:4R axis pointing in opposite directions, one for a small molecule pushing exon 10 out of a 4R-excess model and one for trans-splicing pushing it in in a 3R-excess model, to show that the target is the balance and not a direction.

[NEU-120] @muniz2022smarttau (2022) conf=high
  The isoform balance can be corrected after symptoms have already appeared: htau mice, which over-produce 3R tau, were injected bilaterally at six months of age into either the medial prefrontal cortex or the striatum with trans-splicing molecules driving MAPT exon 10 inclusion and assessed at twelve months; novel-object discrimination in the cortex-injected group returned to the level of age-matched wild-type controls, against a control-vector group that stayed impaired or worsened, and striatum-injected animals had significantly fewer rotarod falls than at six months.

[NEU-121] @muniz2022smarttau (2022) conf=high
  The behavioural rescue tracked the isoform ratio rather than the amount of tau: in both the medial prefrontal cortex and the striatum of trans-splicing-injected htau mice, the ratio of exon-10-containing to exon-10-lacking MAPT transcript rose and the 4R to 3R protein ratio rose above one, against control htau mice whose ratio stayed below one, with no difference in total tau between groups and a significant reduction in tau phosphorylated at Ser202 and at Ser396/404.

## Therapeutic targeting of tau: a named drug in trial

[NEU-124] @nct05399888celia (2026) conf=high
  A tau-directed antisense oligonucleotide has reached a phase 2 efficacy trial, but it lowers tau rather than redirecting its splicing: 416 participants aged 50 to 80 with mild cognitive impairment due to Alzheimer disease or mild Alzheimer dementia were randomised in CELIA, under a quadruple-masked parallel-group design, either to intrathecal placebo every 12 weeks or to diranersen at a low dose every 24 weeks, a high dose every 24 weeks, or a high dose every 12 weeks, for up to 72 weeks, the primary endpoint being a dose response measured as change from baseline at week 76 on the Clinical Dementia Rating sum of boxes.

[NEU-125] @shulman2026biib080phase1b (2026) conf=high
  The trial that preceded it was small and its clinical signal was exploratory: in a multicentre randomised double-blind phase 1b study in mild Alzheimer disease, participants received intrathecal placebo or the MAPT-targeting oligonucleotide BIIB080 at 10 mg (6 participants), 30 mg (6) or 60 mg (9) every four weeks or 115 mg (13) every 12 weeks, with a long-term extension at 60 mg (7) or 115 mg (9) every 12 weeks; post-hoc analyses reported a consistent trend of slowed decline on cognitive, functional and global measures favouring the high-dose groups, alongside reductions from baseline in neurofibrillary tangles on tau positron emission tomography.

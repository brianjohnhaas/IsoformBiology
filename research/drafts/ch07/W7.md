## Antisense oligonucleotide therapy and n-of-1 cases

[NEU-037] @kim2019milasen (2019) conf=high  [ABSTRACT ONLY]
  The endpoint of isoform-level diagnosis is a drug designed for one genome: cell lines from a single patient supplied the proof-of-concept experiments on which milasen, a splice-modulating oligonucleotide, was taken into an n-of-1 study within one year of first contact with that patient.
  FIGURE: A timeline from first clinical contact through variant identification, splicing assay, oligonucleotide design, toxicology and first dose, with the elapsed time at each step - the figure's point being which steps are compressible and which are not.

[NEU-038] @wiltonclark2024nof1aso (2024) conf=medium
  The clinical result of the milasen n-of-1 was partial and should be reported as such: seizure frequency and duration fell to half their pre-treatment values, but the drug did not impede progressive brain volume loss and the patient died three years after treatment began.

[NEU-322] @wiltonclark2024nof1aso (2024) conf=medium
  On the account of a 2024 review, the lesion milasen was built against sat on the second MFSD8 (CLN7) allele, a novel 2 kilobase insertion in intron 6 that redirected splicing so intron 6 sequence entered the mature message, while the first allele carried a known pathogenic variant, and the oligonucleotide was aimed at the cryptic splice site that insertion created.

[NEU-323] @wiltonclark2024nof1aso (2024) conf=medium
  The design route was prediction, then patient cells, then rodent toxicology: candidate 2'-methoxyethyl and 2'-O-methyl oligonucleotides were chosen using in silico predictions, screened in the patient's own fibroblasts for the proportion of healthy CLN7 transcript, and tested in rats at 2.5, 10 and 42 times the intended dose for 70 days, where the high-dose animals showed dorsal root ganglion toxicity and gait disturbance that then shaped clinical monitoring.

[NEU-324] @kim2019milasen (2019) conf=high  [ABSTRACT ONLY]
  The first report of the milasen trial recorded no serious adverse events and a fall in seizures that it called objective, ascertained both from electroencephalography and from what the parent reported, in a single-patient design with no control arm and no comparison other than the patient's own pre-treatment state.

[NEU-329] @nakayama2026kcnt1aso (2026) conf=high
  Individualised oligonucleotide treatment has already run into serious harms, whether or not the drug caused them: under one investigational protocol for a knockdown oligonucleotide in KCNT1 epileptic encephalopathy, which two patients joined ten months apart, the first was found to have severe communicating hydrocephalus after symptoms that began eight weeks past her ninth intrathecal dose, her family then withdrew in favour of palliative care and she died three months after the final dose, while the second developed status dystonicus and needed a ventriculoperitoneal shunt, after which dosing was paused and resumed only two years later at lower doses.

[NEU-330] @kim2023asoframework (2023) conf=high
  Correction was partial and came with an unwanted second effect: in fibroblasts from a patient with the ATM c.7865C>T allele, the two lead oligonucleotides raised functional ATM transcript from 0 to 5 per cent untreated to 29 per cent for AT008 and 18 per cent for AT026, while both also increased skipping of exon 53. An oligonucleotide designed against intronic silencers was built to explore whether that side effect could be avoided and showed no promising activity at all, so the strategy went untested rather than failing.

[NEU-331] @kim2023asoframework (2023) conf=high
  The splice-switching n-of-1 drug that followed milasen has three years of reported tolerability: atipeksen was given intrathecally under an expanded access protocol to a child with ataxia-telangiectasia from the age of two, with exploratory dose escalation and then 42 milligrams every three months, and the investigational treatment had been tolerated for three years without serious adverse events at the time of report.

[NEU-332] @kimmcmanus2026scn2aaso (2026) conf=high
  Published single-patient studies do report missed endpoints: in two parallel n-of-1 studies of individualised allele-selective oligonucleotides in SCN2A developmental and epileptic encephalopathy, each comparing one patient against their own pre-treatment period, the first patient's estimated 26 per cent reduction in seizure count over 744 days on treatment against 90 days before it did not reach significance (beta minus 0.298, 95 per cent confidence interval minus 0.846 to 0.249, P equals 0.286), while the second patient's estimated 90 per cent reduction over 480 days against 42 days before it did.

[NEU-339] @crooke2024nanorareaso (2024) conf=medium
  The splice-modulating half of this field is thinner than the case reports suggest: the same foundation says that while several of its oligonucleotides in development act by altering RNA splicing, it has not yet accumulated enough clinical exposure to judge whether they help, and the three patients it presents in detail were given allele-selective or RNase H oligonucleotides instead.

## Regulatory and safety frame for one-patient oligonucleotides

[NEU-336] @synofzik2022nof1europe (2022) conf=high
  There is no European route built for a drug made for one person: those authors argue that marketing authorisation under exceptional circumstances is not feasible for a one-patient oligonucleotide, so that the only route left is named-patient supply of an unlicensed medicine, with most of the regulatory oversight resting on national law.

[NEU-337] @fda2021individualizedaso (2021) conf=high
  The United States route is a platform written for exactly this case: FDA draft guidance tells a sponsor-investigator to ask the relevant review division for a pre-investigational-new-drug meeting once both a participant and one or more candidate individualised oligonucleotides, backed by convincing proof-of-concept data, are in hand.

[NEU-338] @crooke2024nanorareaso (2024) conf=medium
  The funnel from application to treatment is narrow and slow: of 173 applications to one foundation for an experimental individualised oligonucleotide by 2 January 2023, 78 were accepted for potential treatment, 57 were declined with the commonest reason being that no antisense approach existed for the mutation, and across the first eight investigational new drug applications the median time from acceptance to first dose was 23 months against the foundation's own 18-month target.

## Which variants are tractable for a splice-switching oligonucleotide

[NEU-325] @kim2023asoframework (2023) conf=high
  Tractability has now been estimated across a whole disease rather than inferred from single cases: whole-genome sequencing of 235 individuals with ataxia-telangiectasia from 209 families found that 22 of the 235 (9 per cent) carried at least one variant the authors judged probably amenable to splice-switching oligonucleotide rescue and 13 (6 per cent) a possibly amenable one, 15 per cent in total.

[NEU-326] @kim2023asoframework (2023) conf=high
  The tractable variants are mostly where exome sequencing cannot see them: 26 of the 36 amenable mutational events (72 per cent) lay more than 20 nucleotides from an exon, and restricting the analysis to exons and their flanking 20 nucleotides dropped the yield of amenable variants to 4 per cent, so genome sequencing returned a 3.5-fold higher therapeutic yield than an exome simulation.
  FIGURE: A single axis of distance from the nearest exon boundary with each amenable variant plotted on it, shaded by whether an exome would have captured it, so the reader sees that the therapeutic opportunity sits in the part of the genome that clinical exomes discard.

[NEU-327] @kim2023asoframework (2023) conf=high
  The published criterion for tractability turns on the direction of the splicing error: a variant that creates a new splice site is the easier case to rescue, because a cryptic site can be shut off by covering it with an oligonucleotide, while a variant that merely weakens exon definition demands that the silencer elements around that exon first be found and blocked.

[NEU-328] @kim2023asoframework (2023) conf=high
  The prediction held up where it was checked: all 11 amenable variants tested, two by RNA sequencing of patient fibroblasts and nine by minigene assay, produced the mis-splicing the taxonomy had predicted, and oligonucleotide screens against six of the probably amenable variants found a correcting oligonucleotide for all six.

[NEU-333] @cheerie2025asoscreening (2025) conf=high
  Screened against published eligibility criteria rather than within one disease, the tractable fraction is small and mostly not splicing: of 532 patients given a diagnosis by clinical genome-wide sequencing at one paediatric hospital over six years, 25 unique variants in 26 patients (4.9 per cent) were eligible or likely eligible for oligonucleotide treatment, by canonical exon skipping in 4, splice correction in 3 and messenger RNA knockdown in 18.

[NEU-334] @cheerie2025asoscreening (2025) conf=high
  The criteria put functional RNA evidence ahead of prediction: the guidelines demand experimental evidence before a variant may be assessed for splice correction, and say a splicing prediction made from sequence is no substitute for it, yet in this cohort such evidence was publicly available for only 8 of the 48 intronic or boundary variants.

[NEU-335] @cheerie2025variantguidelines (2025) conf=high
  The consensus scheme sets a high bar for the top tier: variants are sorted into eligible, likely eligible, unlikely eligible, not eligible and unable to assess, and for splice-correcting oligonucleotides eligible means that an oligonucleotide has already been developed and shown effective, clinically or preclinically, for that specific splice-altering variant.

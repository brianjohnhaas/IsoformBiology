## Cryptic exons as pathology-specific markers

[NEU-144] @brown2022unc13a (2022) conf=high
  The second UNC13-family event failed the test that UNC13A passed: across the New York Genome Center post-mortem RNA sequencing cohort the authors saw no increase in the UNC13B frameshift exon in ALS-TDP or FTLD-TDP tissue, and attribute the null to a shorter UNC13B isoform that contains the same exon and is present in control as well as disease brains, which their cell experiments had not shown.

[NEU-145] @brown2022unc13a (2022) conf=high
  Not every TDP-43-dependent splicing change is disease-specific: both the UNC13A and the UNC13B intron retention events, found in the same knockdown screen as the cryptic exons, were also present in control brains, which left the authors unable to say whether TDP-43 pathology increased them.

[NEU-146] @brown2022unc13a (2022) conf=high
  An orthogonal assay reproduced the specificity without sequencing: in situ hybridisation with the same probe used in cultured neurons found cryptic-exon foci in cortical neurons significantly more often in frontal cortex from nine FTLD-TDP individuals than in five neurologically normal controls or four individuals with tau-pathology FTLD, groups assigned by neuropathological subtype.

[NEU-147] @brown2022unc13a (2022) conf=high
  The cryptic exon is regionally patterned in the way the pathology is: UNC13A cryptic inclusion appeared only in motor cortex and spinal cord from ALS-TDP and in frontal and temporal cortex from FTLD-TDP, and was absent from cerebellum in both disease and control tissue, while the STMN2 cryptic event was the more abundant of the two in spinal cord, with the two comparable in cortex.

## Cryptic-exon products as fluid biomarkers

[NEU-162] @irwin2024crypticfluid (2024) conf=high
  The cryptic peptide is detectable in living patients but in fewer than half of them: in cerebrospinal fluid measured by a sandwich immunoassay against the HDGFL2 cryptic neoepitope, signal above the diluent was found in 19 of 44 individuals with sporadic ALS, 20 of 52 symptomatic C9orf72 repeat carriers and 16 of 44 presymptomatic carriers, against 9 of 62 in a control group of healthy, migraine and normal-pressure-hydrocephalus individuals.
  FIGURE: Proportion positive per group as a dot with a binomial interval, control group leftmost, and the assay's own limit of detection drawn as a horizontal line across a second panel of individual values - so the overlap between groups is visible rather than averaged away.

[NEU-163] @irwin2024crypticfluid (2024) conf=high
  The positivity threshold used to call the biomarker sits below the assay's own detection limit: the limit of detection for the cerebrospinal-fluid signal ratio was calculated as 1.09 and for plasma as 1.11, while individuals were counted as positive at any ratio above 1.

[NEU-164] @irwin2024crypticfluid (2024) conf=high
  In blood the same assay loses its separation: plasma was assayed in 66 samples from 43 C9orf72 carriers alongside 30 disease-control samples, and in the 58 plasma samples with a matched cerebrospinal-fluid measurement the two tracked closely overall and very closely within carriers but not within the 30 controls, while the highest plasma concentrations after symptomatic carriers were those of the normal-pressure-hydrocephalus controls, with no group difference reaching significance.

[NEU-165] @irwin2024crypticfluid (2024) conf=high
  The paper that introduced the cryptic-peptide assay reports no sensitivity and no specificity, and says its control groups may not be true controls, because TDP-43 dysfunction occurs in ageing and in several other diseases and the older normal-pressure-hydrocephalus group was not screened for it.

[NEU-166] @irwin2024crypticfluid (2024) conf=high
  A second group reached the same qualitative result with a different method: the authors of the cerebrospinal-fluid immunoassay report that another laboratory also detected the HDGFL2 cryptic protein in cerebrospinal fluid from presymptomatic C9orf72 repeat carriers using targeted mass spectrometry instead of an antibody.

[NEU-167] @calliari2024hdgfl2 (2024) conf=high
  In brain tissue the cryptic protein separates diagnostic groups well but separates the hardest pair poorly: an immunoassay for the HDGFL2 cryptic protein in amygdala distinguished 67 FTLD-TDP and 70 Alzheimer-with-TDP cases from a control group of 27 cognitively normal individuals, 26 of whom had amygdala available, with areas under the curve of 0.92 and 0.85, while distinguishing Alzheimer-with-TDP from 27 Alzheimer cases without TDP-43 pathology reached only 0.68.

## Restoring a single cryptic-exon target: what it rescues

[NEU-156] @ma2022unc13a (2022) conf=high
  The authors who established the UNC13A event say plainly that correcting it alone may not be enough: they propose that fully restoring TDP-43 function will require repairing more than one cryptic target, and note that UNC13A is expressed far more highly in frontal cortex, at 530.2 transcripts per million, than in spinal cord, at 35.54, which would let STMN2 dominate in lower motor neurons and UNC13A in brain.

[NEU-171] @mallika2026multipletargets (2026) conf=high
  Removing one cryptic exon from the genome did not help: mice in which a 44-base-pair deletion makes the Unc13a cryptic exon impossible, crossed with mice lacking TDP-43 in motor neurons, still lost grip strength and body weight, still lost motor neurons by five months, and still reached hindlimb paralysis between six and eight months, five animals per group.

[NEU-172] @mallika2026multipletargets (2026) conf=high
  Restoring many targets at once did help: a chimeric splicing repressor built from the TDP-43 RNA-recognition domain and RAVER1, delivered intravenously by a blood-brain-barrier crossing virus at six weeks of age to mice lacking TDP-43 in motor neurons, transduced about four fifths of motor neurons, held grip strength and body weight at control levels, attenuated the roughly 50 per cent motor neuron loss, and left all treated animals alive at 110 weeks against end-stage at about 47 weeks in the control-virus arm, fifteen animals per group.
  FIGURE: Two survival curves and, beside them, a bar of surviving motor neurons for each arm, with the single-target genetic deletion plotted as a third arm sitting on top of the untreated curve - the point being that one target moved nothing and several moved everything.

[NEU-173] @joseph2025kcnq2 (2025) conf=high
  A cryptic splicing change can be undone pharmacologically and the cell's electrical behaviour follows: TDP-43 dysfunction causes skipping of the KCNQ2 exon encoding the potassium channel pore, the skipped transcript escapes degradation and is translated into a channel with severely reduced conductance, neurons become intrinsically hyperexcitable, and splice-modulating antisense oligonucleotides dampened that hyperexcitability in both cortical and spinal motor neurons depleted of TDP-43.

## STMN2 cryptic exon in ALS and FTD

[NEU-006] @melamed2019stathmin2 (2019) conf=high
  In STMN2 the pathogenic event is a change in RNA processing rather than a mutation: when TDP-43 levels fall, its binding to sites within the first intron is reduced, a cryptic polyadenylation site there is used, and the gene produces a truncated transcript that is not functional.
  FIGURE: A gene diagram of STMN2 with the normal transcript above and the truncated exon-2a transcript below, and, beside each, what protein it can encode - so the reader sees that one transcript end decides whether the gene yields a functional product.

[NEU-007] @melamed2019stathmin2 (2019) conf=high
  Exon 2a was present in laser-captured lumbar motor neurons from every one of thirteen sporadic ALS patients examined and absent from all seven non-ALS individuals tested.

[NEU-008] @melamed2019stathmin2 (2019) conf=high
  Neither the cryptic polyadenylation signal nor the three GU-rich TDP-43 binding sites of human STMN2 intron 1 exist in the corresponding mouse intron, and the authors note that the abnormal processing is correspondingly not recapitulated in TDP-43 transgenic or TDP-43-deficient mice.

[NEU-009] @melamed2019stathmin2 (2019) conf=high
  The splicing lesion carries the phenotype rather than merely accompanying it: re-expressing stathmin-2 from a construct lacking the cryptic polyadenylation site restored axonal regeneration after axotomy in TDP-43-depleted human iPSC motor neurons, even though TDP-43 affects the levels or splicing of many RNAs.

## TDP-43 autoregulation and the loss-of-function model

[NEU-176] @koyama2016tardbpauto (2016) conf=high
  TDP-43 holds its own transcript down by choosing where it ends: TDP-43 suppresses use of the most proximal of the three polyadenylation signals in TARDBP and promotes splicing of alternative introns within its last exon, which routes the message to nonsense-mediated decay. In ALS motor neurons, especially those with TDP-43 mislocalised out of the nucleus, the significant change was in the proportion of TARDBP message that was cytoplasmic, in two of three genotype groups: total message did not increase, and the nuclear and cytoplasmic amounts did not change significantly on their own.

[NEU-177] @dinh2025tardbputr (2026) conf=high
  The autoregulatory element is load-bearing in vivo: deleting the part of the mouse Tardbp 3' untranslated region that holds the TDP-43 binding region, the polyadenylation signals and the alternative introns lowered Tardbp message and was embryonically lethal after gastrulation when homozygous, while aged heterozygotes developed mild locomotor dysfunction with a modest rise in spinal cord TDP-43 protein and fewer motor neurons.

[NEU-178] @ruf2026celltypetdp43 (2026) conf=high
  TDP-43 pathology is not spread evenly across the cortex: in post-mortem motor cortex from 30 ALS, 20 ALS-FTD and 32 control samples, sorting nuclei and combining single-nucleus chromatin and RNA sequencing with spatial transcriptomics put the pathology mainly in excitatory neurons, and named intratelencephalic and extratelencephalic subtypes as the worst affected, with cryptic exon inclusion hitting different gene sets in different cell types.

[NEU-179] @vanzuiden2025gainoffunction (2025) conf=high
  The loss-of-function account is contested, and the cryptic-exon story assumes one side of it: forcing TDP-43 into the cytoplasm with a mutated localisation signal in iPSC-derived neurons shifted APP splicing, with use of the junction specific to the 695-residue isoform falling by 32 per cent and the 751-specific junction rising by 24 per cent. Neither TDP-43 knockdown in iPSC-derived neurons nor complete knockout in HeLa cells produced the shift, and both of those nulls are this paper's reanalyses of other groups' datasets rather than its own experiments.

## TDP-43 cryptic exons as a disease mechanism

[NEU-005] @ling2015crypticexons (2015) conf=high  [ABSTRACT ONLY]
  TDP-43's role here is negative: in mouse embryonic stem cells depleted of it, non-conserved cryptic exons that it normally suppresses entered mRNAs, frequently disrupting translation and triggering nonsense-mediated decay, and enforcing repression prevented the cell death that followed. Those authors suggest that this repression is already impaired in ALS and frontotemporal dementia cases.

## TDP-43 dose thresholds for cryptic exon detection

[NEU-141] @brown2022unc13a (2022) conf=high
  The two cryptic events have different detection points in one knockdown series: in SH-SY5Y neuroblastoma cells carrying a doxycycline-inducible short hairpin against TDP-43, the UNC13A cryptic exon became detectable once more than 50 per cent of TDP-43 had been lost, while the analogous frameshift exon in the sister gene UNC13B was not robustly detected until more than 90 per cent had gone. In the same paper's other series, cryptic exon expression instead correlated with the degree of knockdown, and those authors attribute the apparent floor to nonsense-mediated decay rather than to a threshold in splicing.
  FIGURE: Residual TDP-43 on the x axis from 100 per cent down to zero, with cryptic-event inclusion on the y axis, one curve per event, and the point at which each curve leaves the detection floor marked - so the reader sees that different targets have different switch points rather than one shared dose response.

[NEU-142] @brown2022unc13a (2022) conf=high
  The apparent dose threshold is partly an artefact of transcript destruction: treating the most weakly knocked-down SH-SY5Y cells with cycloheximide, which stalls translation and so blocks nonsense-mediated decay, made the UNC13A cryptic exon detectable where it had not been before, so the cryptic transcript is present below the nominal threshold and is being degraded rather than not made.

## The TDP-43 cryptic splicing catalogue and its reproducibility

[NEU-143] @brown2022unc13a (2022) conf=high
  Different cryptic targets of the same repressor meet different fates: knocking down the nonsense-mediated decay factor UPF1, or inhibiting translation with cycloheximide, raised the levels of the UNC13A cryptic exon transcript and the UNC13B frameshift exon transcript but left the aberrant STMN2 transcript unchanged, which has no predicted premature termination codon.

[NEU-157] @seddighi2024crypticpeptides (2024) conf=high
  Two thirds of a cell-model cryptic-splicing catalogue reappears in human tissue: of 340 pathological splice junctions found in TDP-43-depleted iPSC-derived glutamatergic neurons, 230 were also seen in published RNA sequencing of neuronal nuclei sorted by flow cytometry from FTD and ALS cortex, and 122 of those were enriched in the nuclei that had lost TDP-43.
  FIGURE: A three-column Sankey from events found in the cell model, through events seen at all in sorted patient nuclei, to events enriched in TDP-43-negative nuclei, with the dropped fraction at each step labelled - so the reader sees attrition rather than a single count.

[NEU-158] @humphrey2017crypticquant (2017) conf=high
  Two cryptic-exon screens of the same cell line barely agree: a reanalysis pipeline applied to two ENCODE TDP-43 knockdown datasets in K562 cells classified 95 human cryptic exons in total but found only 11 of them in both datasets, and recovered 12 of the 26 human cryptic exons the original 2015 report had named.

[NEU-159] @jeong2017celltypecryptic (2017) conf=high
  Which cryptic exons appear depends on the cell as much as on TDP-43: comparing conditional Tdp-43 deletions in mouse excitatory neurons, skeletal myocytes and stem cells, only 66 of 221 cryptic exons were shared by at least two of the three cell types and only 32 by all three, leaving 155 found in one cell type alone.

[NEU-160] @vanzuiden2025gainoffunction (2025) conf=high
  Most of the mis-splicing seen in diseased brain is not reproduced by removing TDP-43: only 16 per cent of the transcripts mis-spliced in TDP-43-negative neuronal nuclei from ALS and FTLD-TDP post-mortem brain were also mis-spliced after TDP-43 knockdown in cultured neurons, which those authors take as evidence that loss of nuclear function cannot be the whole mechanism.

[NEU-161] @irwin2024crypticfluid (2024) conf=high
  The paper's introduction restates from two earlier studies that about 3 per cent of the non-conserved cryptic exons appearing when TDP-43 splicing repression is lost are spliced in frame, and so produce a neoepitope rather than a frameshift or a premature stop. That fraction is the denominator a cryptic-peptide biomarker programme works inside, and it is a restatement rather than a measurement made here.

## UNC13A cryptic exon and the ALS GWAS signal

[NEU-010] @brown2022unc13a (2022) conf=high
  Cryptic exon de-repression on TDP-43 loss is broad but only rarely lands on a known risk gene - of 179 cryptic exons detected in TDP-43-depleted iPSC-derived cortical-like neurons, the synaptic gene UNC13A was the only host gene that was also an established ALS-FTD risk gene.

[NEU-011] @brown2022unc13a (2022) conf=high
  In 1,349 post-mortem brain and spinal cord tissues from 377 individuals, the UNC13A cryptic exon was found only in TDP-43 proteinopathy - in 89 per cent of FTLD-TDP and 38 per cent of ALS-TDP individuals - and in no SOD1 or FUS ALS case, no FTLD-TAU or FTLD-FUS case, and no control.
  FIGURE: A matrix of diagnostic group against cryptic-exon detection rate, including the non-TDP neurodegenerative groups as explicit zeroes, so the specificity of the marker to TDP-43 pathology is the visual message.

[NEU-012] @brown2022unc13a (2022) conf=high
  The long-unexplained UNC13A GWAS association resolves into a splicing effect: patient tissues homozygous for the risk alleles showed significantly more UNC13A cryptic exon inclusion even after normalising to STMN2 cryptic splicing, which controls for how much TDP-43 function each sample had already lost.

[NEU-013] @ma2022unc13a (2022) conf=high
  The top ALS- and FTD-associated variants at UNC13A lie not in coding sequence but inside the intron that harbours the cryptic exon, and the authors show they increase cryptic exon splicing when TDP-43 is dysfunctional - a direct functional route from a GWAS hit to a splicing change.

## UNC13A risk variants and the causal splicing step

[NEU-148] @brown2022unc13a (2022) conf=high
  Brown and colleagues placed the two UNC13A risk variants on the splicing pathway with minigenes rather than with association: constructs spanning exon 20, intron 20 and exon 21 showed that the cryptic-exon variant and, less strongly, the intronic variant each promoted cryptic inclusion on their own in TDP-43-depleted SH-SY5Y cells, with the highest inclusion from the construct carrying both risk alleles.

[NEU-149] @brown2022unc13a (2022) conf=high
  The risk alleles redistribute TDP-43 along the pre-mRNA rather than simply reducing its binding: iCLIP of TDP-43 in HEK 293T cells expressing the risk or the reference minigene found crosslinks concentrated in an approximately 800-nucleotide UG-rich stretch holding both variants, with a 21 per cent fall in crosslinks around the cryptic-exon variant and a 73 per cent rise upstream of the intronic variant.

[NEU-150] @brown2022unc13a (2022) conf=high
  Two independent measurements in the same paper tie the risk allele to TDP-43 binding: isothermal titration calorimetry with recombinant TDP-43 gave a higher dissociation constant for the cryptic-exon risk sequence than for the reference sequence, and mutating the UGNNUG TDP-43 binding motif in that region raised cryptic-exon inclusion.

[NEU-151] @brown2022unc13a (2022) conf=high
  Brown and colleagues report the two UNC13A risk alleles as acting independently and additively on cryptic splicing in their minigene assays, and note that when the two variants are not co-inherited, as in east Asian ALS cohorts, the effect is attenuated.

[NEU-152] @ma2022unc13a (2022) conf=high
  Dissecting the risk haplotype one allele at a time showed which variant does the work: in six further minigene reporters carrying every combination of reference and risk alleles at rs12973192, rs12608932 and the repeat insertion rs56041637, the variant lying inside the cryptic exon itself, rs12973192, had the strongest single effect on inclusion in TDP-43-knockout HEK 293T cells, the other two contributed non-additively, and the largest effect came from carrying all three.
  FIGURE: Eight minigene constructs as rows, the three variant positions as columns filled or empty for risk or reference, and a single bar of cryptic inclusion beside each row - so the reader can see the non-additivity directly instead of being told about it.

[NEU-153] @ma2022unc13a (2022) conf=high
  The two companion papers disagree about the biochemistry: by electrophoretic mobility shift assay with full-length recombinant TDP-43, Ma and colleagues found the protein bound the reference and risk versions of the cryptic-exon probe with similar affinity, with a minor reduction in affinity for the risk allele and a larger reduction for the repeat variant rs56041637, and they say in the paper that this differs from what Brown and colleagues reported and attribute the difference to their use of full-length protein against RNA-recognition motifs alone.

[NEU-154] @ma2022unc13a (2022) conf=high
  The allele dose shows up in brain tissue as well as in reporters: among 86 FTLD-TDP individuals from the Mayo Clinic Brain Bank who carried the cryptic exon, 85 of whom had matching allele counts at both risk positions, the number of risk haplotypes correlated with cryptic-exon abundance measured by RT-qPCR, with a regression coefficient of 0.175 at P equal to 0.0290.

[NEU-155] @ma2022unc13a (2022) conf=high
  Whether the UNC13A risk variants act additively is unsettled: the minigene dissection in one of the two 2022 papers found the other variants contributing in a non-additive way on top of the cryptic-exon variant, while the other paper's discussion describes the two variants it tested as acting independently and additively.

## Why the mouse does not show the human cryptic-exon programme

[NEU-168] @humphrey2017crypticquant (2017) conf=high
  The non-conservation is total rather than partial: a single pipeline applied to three human and three mouse TDP-43 depletion datasets found no cryptic splicing event shared between the two species, which is the generalisation of the STMN2 case rather than an exception to it.

[NEU-169] @humphrey2017crypticquant (2017) conf=high
  Cryptic exons are not weakly conserved sequence but unconserved sequence: per-exon conservation scores for TDP-43-repressed cryptic exons were indistinguishable from randomly sampled intronic sequence in the same genes and far below the adjacent annotated exons. Their splice sites are weaker than those of the annotated neighbours, which are on average significantly stronger, and at the same time far above random, which those authors read as real but weaker sites.
  FIGURE: Three stacked distributions of per-exon conservation score - annotated exons, cryptic exons, sampled intronic sequence - with the cryptic and intronic distributions drawn overlapping, which is the whole point.

[NEU-170] @mallika2026multipletargets (2026) conf=high
  Even where a mouse has a cryptic exon in the same gene it is not the same lesion: the TDP-43-regulated cryptic exon in mouse Unc13a sits at a different position from the human one, Unc13a protein is much lower in mouse spinal cord than in mouse brain, and RNA sequencing of spinal cord from mice lacking TDP-43 in motor neurons did not detect the Unc13a cryptic exon at all.
